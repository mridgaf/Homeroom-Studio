# Chapter 13: College theory for MIDI patterns
## Part 1 of 4

_Free college-level music theory, graded for trust, then turned into rules a MIDI pattern can follow. Written 2026-10-19._

**In this part:** how good each source is, what was read, what was NOT read.
**Next:** Part 2 of 4.md (chord loops and voice leading)

## The short version
- The best free college source for beat-making is **Open Music Theory** (OMT). It has a Popular Music unit (chord loops, modes, drum beats) and a Jazz unit (voicings, ii–V–I, blues).
- Its authors are professors. It cites peer-reviewed papers. It is free and open (CC BY-SA).
- Berklee's free pages agree with OMT on voice leading. They are thinner.
- The "what does this scale FEEL like" lists are teaching shorthand, not science. They are marked that way below.

## Grades (A = trust, B = good but thin, C = a hint only)
| Grade | Source | Who wrote it | What it gave us | Read how much |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Open Music Theory v2, Pop + Jazz + Modes + Rhythm chapters | Gotham, Gullings, Hamm, Hughes, Jarvis, Lavengood, Peterson (university music theory faculty). Cites Biamonte 2010, Spicer 2017, McClimon 2017, Doll 2017. | Chord-loop schemas, modes, drum beats, tresillo, syncopation, swing, jazz voicings, blues, ii–V–I | 20 chapters read in full |
| B+ | Berklee Online "Voice Leading Paradigms" (2023) | Excerpt of Berklee Online courses (Russell, Kirby, Shim, Schmeling) | Voice-leading rules. They match OMT. | Read in full |
| B | Berklee College, "Essential Features of Hip-Hop Production" (2022) | Prince Charles Alexander, Berklee professor. It is a short excerpt promoting his book. | Hip-hop tempo ranges, kick/hat feel, density | Read in full (short) |
| C+ | Ethan Hein, "Scales and emotions" (2010) and "Modes from light to dark" (2021) | NYU music-tech professor. In the comments he says the emotion list has "no references"; it is his own description of common practice. | Mood words for scales. "Dorian for hip-hop." | Read in full |

## Found but NOT used (so you know)
- **UC Berkeley:** only the course catalog. It lists Music 172 "Popular Music Theory" and Music 151A–D Harmony, but no free lecture notes or readings. Nothing to extract.
- **MIT OpenCourseWare 21M.301 / 302:** only the lecture calendar was read. It is classical harmony and counterpoint. Good for classical voice leading, not read further because it does not cover loops or grooves.
- **Berklee paid courses and textbooks (Music Theory 101/201, Schmeling's book):** paywalled. Not accessible.
- **Puget Sound "Music Theory for the 21st-Century Classroom" (open four-semester textbook):** found, not read.
- **Adams, "Metrical Techniques of Flow in Rap Music" (MTO) and a CUNY dissertation on hip-hop form:** found, not read. Both are about rapping and song form, not beat patterns.
- **Berklee Online handbook PDF:** fetched, but it is a course catalog. Not used.
- Reddit, Facebook, YouTube, and "AI SEO" pages were thrown out.

## Things checked and found wrong or loose
1. OMT's jazz-voicing chapter says the 3–7 two-note voicing "yields only fourths and fifths." On a dominant chord the 3rd and 7th are a **tritone** (G7: B and F). I checked this. The chapter is loose here; the voicing itself is still right.
2. OMT says the lydian shuttle (I–II♯) is **rare** in real songs and easy to confuse with IV–V or ♭VII–I. A MIDI generator should not call a plain I–II loop "lydian" unless the melody also uses the raised 4th.
3. OMT's pentatonic chapter has no worksheets yet ("Coming soon!"). The theory is cited, but the teaching side is unfinished.
4. Hein's mood words are one person's description. Use them to pick a starting colour, then trust your ears.
