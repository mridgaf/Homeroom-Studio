# Panic-Whistle Slide Bounce — sounds like JID "151 Rum" (DiCaprio 2)

**Accuracy: C** — built by ear from the album, not from stems. DiCaprio 2
trades The Never Story's eerie prettiness for panic: a siren-like whistle
figure, a bass that lurches between two notes, and drums built for
double-time flows.

## The Chain
Malström (breathy whistle/flute patch) → The Echo (dotted-8th, driven) + SubTractor 808 (two-note lurch) + tight Kong kit (rimshot-forward) → Scream 4 on the drum bus → dry rap vocal, ad-libs panned wide

## Steps
1. The whistle: Malström, pick a breathy flute/whistle-type patch (Graintable "Flute" family works; so does an NN-XT flute one octave up). Play ONE note (minor 3rd of the key, around C5) in a nervous rhythm: 16th-16th-rest, repeating. Two-note variation at bar 4 only.
2. Make it a siren: The Echo — dotted-8th, feedback 45–55%, drive ON, repeats filtered down to phone-speaker band (HP 500 Hz, LP 3 kHz). The delay must smear the figure into a continuous anxious loop.
3. The lurch bass: SubTractor sine, Legato ON, Portamento 35–45. Two fingers on the Launchkey's low keys, two notes a whole step or minor 3rd apart — rock between them down-up-down per bar without ever fully lifting, so every move slides. No melody — it's a pendulum, not a bassline.
4. Drums for speed: Kong on the Launchkey pads (Shift + Pad Mode → Drum). Kick pad doubles the bass lurch rhythm exactly. Snare replaced by a RIMSHOT or side-stick on 3 (save the full snare for the hook). Hats at 16ths with velocity sawtoothed — drum them with two alternating fingers on one pad, one hitting softer, and the gallop happens by itself.
5. Rolls as punctuation: end every 4th bar with a 32nd hat roll OR a triplet kick stutter — never both. These are the commas for a double-time verse.
6. Grit: drum bus → Scream 4, Damage type Tape, Damage 25–35, then MClass Maximizer catching ~2 dB. The drums should feel slightly overdriven and two feet in front of everything.
7. Space check: NO pads, NO chords. If the beat feels empty, turn the Echo feedback up 5% instead of adding an instrument.
8. Vocal plan: lead bone-dry and center. Ad-libs ("yeah!", gasps) on a second track panned 60% left/right alternating per bar, low-cut at 400 Hz — the DiCaprio 2 call-and-response.

## Why this works
One repeating high note with heavy delay creates constant tension without occupying the midrange, and the two-note lurching sub gives motion without harmony — so a fast, pitch-bending rapper like JID is the only melodic instrument on the record.

## Order of operations
Bass lurch first (it IS the song's rhythm), kick copies it, whistle on top, hats last. Opposite order from the Never Story recipe — this era is drums-and-bass-led.

## Reason-specific trick
Malström's graintable flutes get the breathy, slightly synthetic whistle faster than any sampler. Route the whistle through The Echo's DRIVE stage rather than adding a separate distortion — one device, and the repeats crunch harder than the dry note, which is exactly the 151 Rum texture.

## RE upgrade path
None required. A tape-stop / stutter RE on the whistle track for one beat before each hook is a cheap authenticity boost if you own one.

## Technique you just learned
One-note tension: when the vocalist is the melody, the beat's job is a siren, a pendulum, and a gallop — three rhythms, zero chords.

## Reference
JID — "151 Rum" (DiCaprio 2, 2018): https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=JID+151+Rum+official

# Eerie Music-Box Trap — sounds like JID "NEVER" (The Never Story)

**Accuracy: C** — built by ear from the album, not from an interview or stem
breakdown. The devices and moves below get you the *shape* of the Never
Story sound: eerie toy-box melody, sliding sub, and a beat with holes in it.

## The Chain
Klang Tuned Percussion (music-box patch) → The Echo (dark ping-pong) → RV7000 (small dark plate) + SubTractor 808 (portamento) + sparse Kong kit through ReGroove → dry upfront rap vocal

## Steps
1. The melody: Klang Tuned Percussion, pick a music-box / celesta-type patch. Write a 4-bar minor-key line high up (C5-C6), only 5–7 notes, with at least one flat-2 or tritone in it — pretty but wrong is the target. If a Klang patch search comes up empty, an NN-XT celesta or mallet patch does the same job.
2. Detune the innocence away: clone the Klang track, drop the copy 12 semitones and nudge its fine-tune −10 cents. Mix the low copy 6 dB under the top one. The slight beating between them is the "eerie" part.
3. Space: The Echo after Klang — 1/4 note, feedback ~35%, ping-pong ON, filter the repeats dark (LP around 2 kHz). The delay tail must be doing more work than the melody.
4. The 808: SubTractor sine (Osc 1 waveform 4), Polyphony 1, Legato ON, Portamento 45–55. Play it on the Launchkey KEYS, low octave: hold the first note and press the next one WHILE still holding — that overlap is what makes it slide. Write the bassline so at least one note per bar slides a fourth or more. (Same engine as the Trap 808 recipe; the Never Story flavor is longer slides, fewer notes.)
5. Drums with holes: Kong or Redrum. Finger them on the Launchkey PADS (Shift + Pad Mode → Drum, kick at bottom-left) instead of mouse-clicking — record a loose take, then delete hits until only 3 or 4 kicks per 2 bars remain, never on every downbeat. Snare or rim on 3 (half-time feel). Hats: closed 8ths but MUTE 2–3 of them per bar. The silence is where JID's triplet flows live.
6. Groove: whole drum bus through one ReGroove channel, shuffle 55–60%, slide a few ticks late. Loose, not drunk.
7. Glue and grit: drum bus → Scream 4, Tape, Damage 15–20. MClass Compressor on the mix bus, 4:1, ~2 dB reduction.
8. Vocal: dry and dead-center (Upfront Rap Vocal recipe). Double only the last word of every 4th bar, panned hard both sides — the Never Story ad-lib move.

## Why this works
Every element is small: tiny melody, few drums, one sliding sub. Fast, dense rapping needs a sparse beat the way a solo needs a rhythm section — the vocal IS the busy part, and the eerie toy-box top keeps the mood dark without filling the midrange the voice needs.

## Order of operations
Melody first (it sets the key for the 808 slides), then the 808 line, then drums LAST — place kicks only where the bass isn't sliding.

## Reason-specific trick
Klang is the platform advantage: it's a physical-model of exactly the struck-metal timbres this era of Atlanta production sampled. Save Klang + The Echo + RV7000 as a Combinator ("Music Box Dark.cmb") — that's your Never Story template starter.

## RE upgrade path
None needed — all stock Reason 10+ devices. If you own a granular RE, a frozen grain pad 20 dB under everything adds the album's "haunted room" floor.

## Technique you just learned
Negative space: mute drum hits instead of adding them — a hole in the beat is an instrument when the rapper is fast enough to fill it.

## Reference
JID — "NEVER" (The Never Story, 2017): https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=JID+NEVER+official

# Kick and 808 Relationship — make them hit as one

**Accuracy: B** — the three methods are standard. The exact numbers are starting points to adjust by ear. Assumes a clean kick one-shot and a clean sine-based 808 with no built-in sidechain or saturation.

## The Chain
Kick → mixer. 808 → MClass Equalizer → MClass Compressor (sidechain fed by the kick) → mixer. Wrap the 808 chain in a Combinator.

## Steps
1. Why you're doing this: kick and 808 both live around 40–80 Hz. Together untreated they mask each other and the low end turns muddy or the kick disappears on small speakers.
2. Method 1, duck the 808 (most common). Flip the rack (Tab). Split the kick with a Spider Audio Merger & Splitter: one copy to the mixer, one into the Sidechain input of the MClass Compressor on the 808. Turn the compressor's Sidechain button ON.
3. Compressor settings for the duck: ratio 4:1, threshold about -18 dB until you see 4–6 dB reduction on each kick, attack 0.1 ms, release 80–120 ms, soft knee ON. The 808 gets out of the way for a split second on each kick.
4. Method 2, split the frequencies (use when the 808 sustains and ducking would sound wobbly). Kick EQ: -6 dB shelf at 50 Hz, +3 dB at 90 Hz, +2 dB at 3 kHz. 808 EQ: +2 dB shelf at 40 Hz, -4 dB at 90 Hz, roll off above 800 Hz.
5. Method 3, both gently (the usual default). EQ the 808 like Method 2 but with a -2 dB cut at 90 Hz, then compress with only 2–3 dB of duck. Put the EQ first and the compressor after.
6. Wrap the 808 EQ and compressor in a Combinator. Knobs: Rotary 1 = duck amount, 2 = sub boost, 3 = release, 4 = mud cut at 90 Hz, Button 1 = bypass so you can A/B.
7. Check: play the same rhythm with the Combinator bypassed and on. Bypassed should sound smaller and muddier. If not, check the cable is really patched, the 808 has no built-in enhancer, and the kick tail isn't too long.

## Why this works
Ducking handles the moment of the hit, frequency splitting handles the steady sound. Together your ear hears one low-end event.

## Order of operations
EQ first to remove overlap, compressor after to handle what's left. Don't shape tone before the duck is set.

## Reason-specific trick
Save as "KickPocket 808 Bus.cmb". Stock Reason can only duck the whole 808, not just part of it. If the kick has a long tail, shorten the sample or tighten it with a gate first, otherwise the 808 pumps too long.

## RE upgrade path
Only a multiband sidechain would fix the "whole 808 ducks" limit. The old notes named Kilohearts Multipass and FabFilter Pro-MB, with prices from May 2026. Unverified, check before buying anything.

## Technique you just learned
Sharing a crowded frequency range between two sources. The same moves work for kick plus bass guitar, kick plus synth bass, floor tom plus bass.

## Reference
Any modern trap track, solo the kick and 808 to hear the duck: https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=kick+808+sidechain+tutorial

# Lo-Fi Sample Chop — dusty soul-loop texture

**Accuracy: B** — the filtering/saturation half is fully stock; real vinyl crackle needs a noise sample (easy to add), and tape warble needs a slow pitch LFO trick (step 6).

## The Chain
NN-XT (chops) → MClass EQ (bandwidth cut) → Scream 4 (tape) → CF-101 (subtle warble)

## Steps
1. Chop: load the sample into NN-XT, slice your chops across pads/keys (or use Dr. Octo Rex if it's a REX loop). Root each chop to a comfortable key zone.
2. Play the chops as an instrument — the flip matters more than the effects. Get the pattern right before any processing.
3. MClass EQ: Lo Cut ON, plus high cut — Param 2 at ~9 kHz, -9 dB, wide Q. Old records have no extreme top or bottom; this single move is 50% of "lo-fi."
4. Scream 4: Damage type Tape, Damage 30–45, P2 (speed) low. Compression-like thickening plus soft-clip warmth.
5. Vinyl layer: put an actual vinyl-crackle sample (free online) on its own channel under the loop at -25 dB. Fake crackle from a device fools nobody; a real crackle recording does.
6. Warble: CF-101, rate very slow (~15), delay ~50, feedback 0, sync OFF, dry/wet low. This fakes tape pitch drift. Subtle — if you can point to it, halve it.
7. Bus it with your boom-bap drum bus recipe; shared Scream on the 2-bus marries loop and drums.

## Why this works
Removing highs/lows and adding harmonic clutter mimics generational loss — the ear reads "old media" as "warm and nostalgic."

## Order of operations
EQ before saturation: cutting the top first means Scream's harmonics land in the mids where the warmth lives, not as brittle top.

## Reason-specific trick
NN-XT group feature: set chop groups to mono/legato so overlapping chops cut each other off like an MPC in mono mode.

## RE upgrade path
None required. If chopping is your main workflow, the Beat Map or similar chop-focused RE speeds the workflow but doesn't change the sound.

## Technique you just learned
Bandwidth reduction as a vibe tool — and layering real-world noise instead of synthesizing it.

# Trap 808 — sounds like modern trap sub

**Accuracy: B** — SubTractor builds a legit 808. The missing character is sample-specific distortion from the classic TR-808 bridged-T circuit; saturation gets close.

## The Chain
SubTractor → Scream 4 → MClass Compressor

## Steps
1. SubTractor: Osc 1 waveform 4 (sine). Polyphony 1, Legato ON, Portamento ~40 for the trap slide between notes.
2. Amp envelope: A 0, D 90+, S 0 for boom that decays — or S 80 for held 808s you gate with note length. R ~30.
3. The knock: Mod envelope → destination Osc Pitch, Amount ~40–55, A 0, D ~25, S 0. Every note now starts with a fast pitch drop — that transient IS the knock.
4. Filter 1: LP24, freq ~70%, no resonance. Just taming harmonics from the saturation to come.
5. Scream 4: Damage type Tape, Damage 25–40. This adds harmonics so the 808 is audible on phone speakers. More Damage = more mids = more "distorted 808" as a style choice.
6. MClass Compressor: ratio 4:1, fast attack, ~3 dB reduction — evens out note-to-note level so the sub is constant.
7. Play it low: F1–B1 range. Write the pattern, then tune 808 notes to the song key — an out-of-key 808 ruins everything above it.

## Why this works
An 808 is a sine with a pitch-drop transient; saturation folds sub energy up into audible mids.

## Order of operations
Scream before the compressor: saturation changes level dynamics, and the comp should even out the FINAL sound.

## Reason-specific trick
Combinator 2: map macro 1 to Scream Damage ("dirt"), macro 2 to Mod Env Amount ("knock"). Save as "My 808.cmb". Sidechain note: to duck it under the kick, use the channel compressor's sidechain input fed from the kick channel — Reason's mixer does this natively, no CV hacks needed.

## RE upgrade path
None needed. If you want instant variety, sample packs beat synthesis for exotic 808s.

## Technique you just learned
Transients can be synthesized with pitch envelopes — the same trick makes kicks, toms, and lasers.

# Trap Drum Bus — sounds like Metro Boomin / 21 Savage

**Accuracy: B** — a standard, well-known bus chain adapted to Reason, not a documented session. Assumes decent samples already: punchy kick, crisp clap, dry hats, each on its own track.

## The Chain
All drums → one bus channel → MClass Equalizer → MClass Compressor → Pulveriser (mixed in quietly, about 20%) → master

## Steps
1. Send kick, clap, hats and percussion to one bus channel (the "bus" is one channel that all the drums share, so you treat them together).
2. MClass Equalizer, kick weight: boost +2 dB at 60 Hz, Q 1.2.
3. Same EQ, mud and snap: cut -3 dB at 350 Hz (Q 1.4) to clear the boxy sound, boost +1.5 dB at 5 kHz (Q 0.8) for clap snap, and +1 dB shelf at 10 kHz.
4. MClass Compressor: ratio 2.5:1, attack 10 ms (lets the hit through first), release 100 ms, soft knee ON. Set the threshold so the needle moves 2–3 dB on hits, then raise the output back to the level you started with.
5. Pulveriser for grit: Squash 25, Dirt 15, filter open, volume matched. Blend it in so it's only about 20% of the sound. Dirt adds extra high tones that make the kick and clap thicker without flattening the hit.
6. Check it by muting the Pulveriser. You should lose "thickness" but not the punch. If you lose punch, lower Squash.

## Why this works
EQ first fixes the tone of the whole group, so the compressor reacts to a balanced sound and not a 350 Hz pile-up. The quiet grit adds harmonics without squashing the hits.

## Order of operations
EQ before compressor so it isn't reacting to mud. Grit last and parallel so the dry hit stays intact.

## Reason-specific trick
The bus channel's own Channel Dynamics can do the compression if you want one fewer device. The MClass shows more on its meter. Save the whole chain as "Trap Drum Bus.cmb".

## RE upgrade path
None required. The old notes suggested Softube Saturation Knob on the clap channel for extra crack. Unverified, check it exists in your Reason before relying on it.

## Technique you just learned
Bus-level EQ before glue compression. Works on any drum bus in any genre.

## Reference
21 Savage — "Bank Account": https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=21+Savage+Bank+Account+official

# Trap Hats with Pitch Rolls — sounds like Migos "Bad and Boujee"

**Accuracy: B** — the roll-and-pitch trick is widely used. Assumes one clean, dry closed hi-hat sample.

## The Chain
Dr. Octo Rex (or NN-XT) with a hi-hat → MClass Equalizer → mixer channel

## Steps
1. Load the hi-hat into Dr. Octo Rex. Pitch 0 (you'll automate it later). High-pass filter at 800 Hz to cut low rumble. Amp envelope A 0, D 60 ms, S 0, R 40 ms.
2. MClass Equalizer: cut -6 dB shelf at 200 Hz, cut -2 dB at 4 kHz (calms harsh mids), boost +3 dB shelf at 10 kHz (sizzle).
3. Program a steady 16th-note hat pattern in the sequencer.
4. Add triplet rolls at random spots: three hits squeezed into the space of two normal 16ths.
5. Record automation on the Dr. Octo Rex transpose (pitch) control. During each roll go up 2–4 semitones, then snap back to 0.
6. Randomize velocity between 80 and 127 so it doesn't sound machine-perfect. On the Launchkey pads you can also just finger-drum the roll.

## Why this works
Hats are sparkle, not low end, so cutting lows keeps them out of the kick and snare space. The pitch climbing during a roll gives a "lift" that makes it sound expensive.

## Order of operations
EQ after the sample player so you shape the sound after its envelope.

## Reason-specific trick
Draw the pitch automation in the sequencer lane for the Dr. Octo Rex pitch control. Save the device settings as "Trap Hat.cmb".

## RE upgrade path
None required.

## Technique you just learned
Pitch automation during drum rolls. Works on snare rolls and fills too.

# Trap Snare and Clap Layer — sounds like Mike WiLL Made-It

**Accuracy: B** — two samples stacked is a standard trick. The final sound depends on which two samples you pick. Assumes one dry clap (the body) and one snappy snare (the snap).

## The Chain
Combinator [NN-XT clap + NN-XT snare → Combinator mixer → MClass Equalizer → MClass Compressor] → mixer channel

## Steps
1. Make a Combinator and put two NN-XTs in it. Load the clap in the first and the snare in the second.
2. Clap NN-XT: pitch 0, filter fully open, amp envelope A 0, D 80 ms, S 0, R 50 ms (short, no tail).
3. Snare NN-XT: pitch 0 (or +2 semitones for crispness), high-pass filter at 120 Hz so it leaves the low body to the clap, amp envelope A 0, D 30 ms, S 0, R 30 ms.
4. In the Combinator's mixer: clap at 0 dB, snare at -4 dB (the snare is the helper, not the lead). Both centered.
5. MClass Equalizer on the Combinator output: +1 dB at 200 Hz (body), -2 dB at 1.5 kHz (removes honk), +3 dB at 6 kHz (crack), +2 dB shelf at 12 kHz (air).
6. MClass Compressor: ratio 6:1, attack 1 ms, release 80 ms, soft knee OFF, about 6 dB reduction on hits. The fast hard squeeze glues the two sounds into one hit.
7. Map four Combinator knobs: Rotary 1 = clap level, 2 = snare level, 3 = the 6 kHz crack, 4 = compressor threshold. Now you can retune per song from the front.

## Why this works
Two samples give you more control than one: the clap carries weight, the snare carries snap. Hard compression makes your ear hear one hit.

## Order of operations
Layer inside the Combinator first, then EQ and compress the combined sound together.

## Reason-specific trick
Save as "Trap Snare Layer.cmb". The same layering works for kicks (sub plus click) and hats (closed plus open).

## RE upgrade path
None required. The old notes named a Kuassa maximizer for a more metallic top. Unverified.

## Technique you just learned
Sample layering inside a Combinator with knob control.

## Reference
Mike WiLL Made-It — "23": https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Mike+WiLL+Made-It+23+official
