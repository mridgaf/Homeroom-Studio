# Free learning resources (real courses, no cost)

These are the well-known, genuinely free (or free-to-audit) places to build
theory and production knowledge beyond what these voice notes cover — good
for a rainy day when you want to go deeper than a "what is X" answer.

## Full courses

- **Berklee — "Introduction to Music Production" (Coursera, audit free)**:
  DAW-agnostic fundamentals — recording, editing, mixing basics (volume,
  pan, sends), dynamics, EQ, delay, synthesis. **Best takeaway**: gain
  staging and signal chain — the actual reason your 808 clips or your vocal
  sits wrong in a mix.
- **BerkleeX — "Introduction to Music Theory" (edX, audit free)**: six
  lessons covering scales, intervals, chords, harmony, ear training, song
  form. **Best takeaway**: knowing minor scales and what each chord's "job"
  is turns a random 2-bar loop into an actual progression.
- **Open Yale Courses — "Listening to Music" (Craig Wright, MUSI 112)**: 23
  full lectures on how music is built — rhythm, form, harmony, bass
  patterns. **Best takeaway**: learning to hear song FORM — why the best
  rap songs still have a real intro/verse/hook architecture underneath.
- **MIT OpenCourseWare — Music & Technology series**: history of recording
  tech and algorithmic/generative music. **Best takeaway**: sampling and
  "the studio as an instrument" have a real lineage (tape experiments →
  hip hop) worth knowing and stealing from.
- **musictheory.net**: free interactive lessons plus ear-training drills and
  calculators. **Best takeaway**: daily 10-minute interval ear-training is
  the fastest way to pick basslines and melodies off records you want to flip.
- **Open Music Theory v2** (free online textbook): a full college theory
  sequence that also has dedicated pop-music and jazz chapters.
  **Best takeaway**: the pop-harmony chapters explain the loop-based
  progressions hip hop actually runs on, in plain terms.

## YouTube channels

- **In The Mix (Michael Wynne)**: beginner-friendly mixing/mastering,
  famous for explaining things clearly. **Best takeaway**: his EQ and
  compression videos are the shortest path to "why doesn't my beat sound
  like the reference track."
- **Produce Like A Pro (Warren Huart)**: full start-to-finish mix
  breakdowns of real songs. **Best takeaway**: watching an entire
  professional mix from raw, unmixed tracks demystifies the whole workflow.
- **Pensado's Place (Dave Pensado)**: interviews with Grammy-winning
  engineers. **Best takeaway**: hearing the actual engineers behind hit
  rap/R&B records explain their vocal chains, in their own words.

## Use it on the Launchkey

Pick ONE resource above that matches what's actually holding your last beat
back — theory gap, mixing gap, or ear-training gap — and do 15 minutes of
it before your next session, not all of them at once.
