# Panic-Whistle Slide Bounce — sounds like JID "151 Rum" (DiCaprio 2)

**Accuracy: C** — built by ear from the album, not from stems. DiCaprio 2
trades The Never Story's eerie prettiness for panic: a siren-like whistle
figure, a bass that lurches between two notes, and drums built for
double-time flows.

## The Chain
Malström (breathy whistle/flute patch) → The Echo (dotted-8th, driven) + SubTractor 808 (two-note lurch) + tight Kong kit (rimshot-forward) → Scream 4 on the drum bus → dry rap vocal, ad-libs panned wide

## Steps
1. The whistle: Malström, pick a breathy flute/whistle-type patch (Graintable "Flute" family works; so does an NN-XT flute one octave up). Play ONE note (minor 3rd of the key, around C5) in a nervous rhythm: 16th-16th-rest, repeating. Two-note variation at bar 4 only.
2. Make it a siren: The Echo — dotted-8th, feedback 45–55%, drive ON, repeats filtered down to phone-speaker band (HP 500 Hz, LP 3 kHz). The delay must smear the figure into a continuous anxious loop.
3. The lurch bass: SubTractor sine, Legato ON, Portamento 35–45. Two fingers on the Launchkey's low keys, two notes a whole step or minor 3rd apart — rock between them down-up-down per bar without ever fully lifting, so every move slides. No melody — it's a pendulum, not a bassline.
4. Drums for speed: Kong on the Launchkey pads (Shift + Pad Mode → Drum). Kick pad doubles the bass lurch rhythm exactly. Snare replaced by a RIMSHOT or side-stick on 3 (save the full snare for the hook). Hats at 16ths with velocity sawtoothed — drum them with two alternating fingers on one pad, one hitting softer, and the gallop happens by itself.
5. Rolls as punctuation: end every 4th bar with a 32nd hat roll OR a triplet kick stutter — never both. These are the commas for a double-time verse.
6. Grit: drum bus → Scream 4, Damage type Tape, Damage 25–35, then MClass Maximizer catching ~2 dB. The drums should feel slightly overdriven and two feet in front of everything.
7. Space check: NO pads, NO chords. If the beat feels empty, turn the Echo feedback up 5% instead of adding an instrument.
8. Vocal plan: lead bone-dry and center. Ad-libs ("yeah!", gasps) on a second track panned 60% left/right alternating per bar, low-cut at 400 Hz — the DiCaprio 2 call-and-response.

## Why this works
One repeating high note with heavy delay creates constant tension without occupying the midrange, and the two-note lurching sub gives motion without harmony — so a fast, pitch-bending rapper like JID is the only melodic instrument on the record.

## Order of operations
Bass lurch first (it IS the song's rhythm), kick copies it, whistle on top, hats last. Opposite order from the Never Story recipe — this era is drums-and-bass-led.

## Reason-specific trick
Malström's graintable flutes get the breathy, slightly synthetic whistle faster than any sampler. Route the whistle through The Echo's DRIVE stage rather than adding a separate distortion — one device, and the repeats crunch harder than the dry note, which is exactly the 151 Rum texture.

## RE upgrade path
None required. A tape-stop / stutter RE on the whistle track for one beat before each hook is a cheap authenticity boost if you own one.

## Technique you just learned
One-note tension: when the vocalist is the melody, the beat's job is a siren, a pendulum, and a gallop — three rhythms, zero chords.
