# Tony Tone Dusty Boom-Bap Drums — sounds like A$AP Rocky, "Tony Tone"

**Accuracy: C** — built by ear and research, not from the session. This is the opposite of crisp modern trap: dusty, dry, a bit lo-fi, late-80s New York sound. If it sounds old next to modern trap, that's correct. Reason's clean stock samples are the main limit. Real vinyl-sourced breaks would help most.

## The Chain
Kick: Kong → MClass Equalizer → Scream 4 (Tape). Snare: Kong → MClass Equalizer → MClass Compressor → Scream 4 (Tape). Hats: Kong → MClass Equalizer. All three → drum bus group → Scream 4 (Tape) → MClass Compressor → MClass Equalizer → channel Drive.

## Steps
1. Pick drums with body and a bit of age, not crisp electronic ones. Kick: pitch -1 semitone, medium-short decay. Snare: pitch -2 semitones, short-ish decay. Hats: closed, short, level -8 dB so they sit behind.
2. Kick EQ: +2 dB shelf at 70 Hz, -2 dB at 250 Hz, +2 dB at 1.8 kHz, and cut -3 dB shelf at 8 kHz. That last cut is the "dusty" move. Then Scream 4 Tape, Damage 18, P1 30, P2 75, Cut Hi 30.
3. Snare EQ: -4 dB shelf at 100 Hz, +2 dB at 200 Hz (body), +3 dB at 3 kHz, -2 dB shelf at 10 kHz. Compressor 3:1, attack 10 ms, release 150 ms. Scream 4 Tape, Damage 22, P1 35, P2 70, Cut Hi 35.
4. Hats EQ: cut -10 dB shelf at 250 Hz, -2 dB at 4 kHz, -3 dB shelf at 12 kHz. Play hats on 8ths with a few 16th rolls, and leave some bars with none.
5. Drum bus: Scream 4 Tape, Damage 15, P1 40, P2 65, Cut Hi 45. Compressor 3:1, attack 20 ms, release 150 ms, 3–5 dB reduction. EQ +1 dB shelf at 60 Hz, -1 dB at 400 Hz, -2 dB shelf at 10 kHz. Channel Drive 12–18%.
6. The groove (half the sound): quantize at 75–85% strength, not 100%, and nudge the snare 5–15 ticks late. Add shuffle around 55–60% if your sequencer has it. Check the exact control names in your Reason.
7. Optional dust: a vinyl crackle loop in its own NN-XT channel at -25 to -30 dB.

## Why this works
Three moves add up: cut the highs on everything, stack many small tape grit stages, and keep the mids forward. Slower compression lets the drums breathe and swing.

## Order of operations
EQ before the grit on each drum so the grit hears the shaped sound. Bus grit, then compressor, then final EQ.

## Reason-specific trick
Wrap the bus chain in a Combinator, knob 1 = Scream Damage (more or less dust), knob 2 = bus compressor threshold. Save as "Dusty Bus.cmb". Finger the drums on the Launchkey pads, or use the Yamaha DTX400K with sticks, for natural looseness.

## RE upgrade path
None required. Authentic drum breaks from old records would help more than any device. The old notes named other saturation REs. Unverified.

## Technique you just learned
Cut highs instead of boosting, stack gentle grit, and loosen the timing. It works for any 90s East Coast revival or lo-fi hip-hop.
