## 1. Groove science says the kick-length order was right

Groove ratings track **spectral FLUX in 50–100 Hz** — change in the low
band, not its level (Stupacher, in research-emotion-science). A long
sustained 808 that never stops moving = LOW flux; short, choked, gappy
subs = HIGH flux. [synthesis]: the owner's "not every beat needs the
long drawn-out 808" is what the groove literature predicts — **short/
varied kick sustain = more urge to move; sustained sub = weight and
dread at the cost of bounce**. Choke length is now a documented
emotion dial, not just variety: dance ↔ choppy sub, menace ↔ held sub.

## 2. Drums-only emotion ladder (the cue ranking, re-derived for us)

The famous cue ranking (mode > tempo > register > dynamics >
articulation > timbre, emotion-theory §1) assumes melody. Drums-only
output has no mode. [synthesis] — for our beats the ladder collapses to:

1. **Tempo/felt pulse** (arousal),
2. **Register** = drum tuning (low dark kicks/snares = heavy-sad; bright
   snaps/hats = playful),
3. **Dynamics as CHANGE** (see §4),
4. **Articulation = choke/decay** (short = staccato = playful-energetic;
   long ringing = legato = heavy-sad) — the kick-length dial again,
5. **Timbre** (dust/distortion = dark-aggressive; clean = bright).

Practical: every crew member's feel can be tuned along these five
without touching a note of harmony. Rage Engine = fast+loud+harsh
(high-arousal negative); Crate Prophet = slow+swung+dark+legato
(low-arousal positive). When he says "make X more menacing," the knobs
are: slower felt pulse, lower tunings, longer subs, more roughness.

## 3. Trap's trick is TWO arousal bands at once

Tempo anchors (emotion-theory §3): 60–80 bpm calm, 120+ excited.
Halftime trap at 140 has a FELT pulse of 70 (calm/heavy band) while the
hat grid subdivides at excited-band rates. [synthesis]: dark-but-
exciting trap emotion = deliberately split arousal — body hears a slow
heavy pulse, ear hears frantic subdivision. Design dial: **subdivision
rate ÷ felt pulse = arousal split**. Rage Engine maxes both ends
(manic); Night Metro keeps pulse low and spends rolls sparingly
(dread with bursts). Boom bap (90 bpm, 8th hats) is UNSPLIT — one calm
band — which is why it reads as settled/head-nod.

## 4. Loudness is flat everywhere in our masters — the science wants contrast

Dynamics are a WEAK static cue but a STRONG change cue (Eerola, via
emotion-theory §1), and sudden dynamic change is a top-3 frisson
trigger (§8). Our −8 LUFS master is statically loud; LUFS gating
ignores quiet bars, so **bar-level RMS contrast is free** — it costs no
loudness. Measured proof already exists: Night Metro's blackout bar sat
5–9 dB under its neighbors while the file still hit −8.9 LUFS.
[synthesis] rule: engineer **≥4–6 dB of bar-RMS swing somewhere in
every 8-bar loop** (drop-out, half-kit bar, velocity dip) — arousal for
free under a constant loudness target.

## 5. A drums-only frisson recipe (three triggers, all already in the toolkit)

The three documented chill triggers (emotion-theory §8) map onto
devices we already ship: (1) new voice after a stripped bar = a stamp
or fx one-shot entering at bar 8 after a bar-7 strip; (2) sudden
dynamic change = §4's RMS swing; (3) unprepared harmony has no drums
equivalent — [synthesis]: its analog is **unprepared TIMBRE**, a hit
from outside the kit's established palette (the fx-替-hats rule).
Stack all three at one moment: strip bar 7 → bar 8 downbeat lands full
kit + a NEW timbre + max velocity. The reversed-cymbal-into-downbeat
trick (forums §8) is the same machine: anticipation → confirmed
arrival (Huron's ITPRA).

## 6. Silence has a dose-response nobody wrote down

Three reports use gaps at different lengths for different feelings:
half-bar gaps = funk bounce (Timbaland, producers), 1–2-beat pre-hook
strip = impact (emotion-production §3), 1–2 bar silence-then-hit =
dread/stinger (horror scoring §1). [synthesis] dose table for the same
device: **0.5–1 beat = bounce · 2–4 beats = anticipation · 4–8 beats =
dread**. Chrome Dial lives at dose 1, Night Metro at dose 2–3. A
character can escalate doses across a beat's B section.

## 7. Why 54% swing feels "loose, not swung" — the sub-perceptual theory

Frane's 30 classic breaks: median 54.5% swing. Linn: 54% "loosens
without sounding swung." At 90 bpm, 54% = 2 MPC ticks ≈ 14 ms — the
same order as human timing jitter (σ 3–12 ms, research-dsp §10).
[synthesis]: era feel lives where the CONSTANT offset is roughly the
size of the human noise floor — big enough to feel, too small to name.
This is also why Dilla's constant lane offsets beat randomization:
**feel = systematic displacement at sub-perceptual scale; noise = the
same magnitude without the consistency.** Vindicates the engine's
LaneFeel design from two independent research threads.

## 8. Euclidean necklaces are a syncopation dial with a known sweet spot

Witek's inverted-U: medium syncopation maximizes groove (emotion-theory
§9). Euclid E(k,n) + rotation sets syncopation almost parametrically
(research-dsp §6) — E(3,8) tresillo is the canonical medium. The Jersey
club kick (x--x--x-x-x-----) is tresillo-family: 3+3+2 logic with a
double-tap. [synthesis]: club kick grammars, trap hats, and dembow all
converge on the SAME 3+3+2 engine from research-theory §7 — meaning New
Math's Euclid perc and Jersey kick aren't two gimmicks but one idea at
two densities, and both sit inside the documented groove sweet spot as
long as the "one" stays anchored. Corollary: pick rotations that avoid
erasing beat 1.

## 9. Boom bap × club fusion is pre-approved by the math

[synthesis] from §7+§8: boom-bap swing (54–58% on 16ths) and tresillo
club kicks are both medium-syncopation devices from different eras.
Swinging the CLUB KICK PATTERN itself (not just hats) at 56–58% and
~92–96 bpm keeps both inside the sweet spot — a fusion the corpus
implies is groovy but documents nowhere. This is New Math's boom-bap
mode with one step further (swing on the kick lane too).

## 10. Margulis's repetition law points at a cheap engine upgrade

Repetition raises liking, but vary TIMBRE across repeats to delay
wear-out (emotion-theory §9). Our beats vary PATTERN across bars (fills)
but hold one kit start-to-finish. [synthesis]: the research-backed
variation axis is the other one — **swap one lane's sample in the B
section** (alternate snare bars 5–8, or hats open up) while patterns
repeat. Small build: per-lane "B-kit" alternates in build_kit.

## 11. Silence as an invitation — the beds have a soloist now (owner, 2026-07-15)

New fact from the owner: every crew beat is a BED — he plays live
instruments over the top. That inverts several findings already in the
corpus without needing any new research:

- Metro's documented signature — "drums hit harder because little
  competes" (producers §Metro) — reads the other way around now: the
  OWNER's instrument hits harder when the drums leave the space. Negative
  space stops being just drama; it's his spotlight.
- Rap melody theory (theory §minor): minor pentatonic loops win because
  they "leave maximal space for the voice." The drums-only analog:
  timekeeper lanes stay subtle and mid-range stays uncrowded so the
  melodic voice on top owns it. (Already house law: hats |pan| ≤ 0.2,
  modest gains.)
- The §6 dose table doubles as a FILL-SIZE table for a live player:
  0.5–1-beat gaps = bounce he can poke notes into; **2–4-beat holes are
  exactly pickup/fill length** — an invitation to answer; 4–8-beat
  strips = space for an exposed phrase (the "solo instrument over sparse
  backing" effect, emotion-production §2).
- Call-and-response placement is already solved: the crew's stamps live
  on the tails of bars 4/8. Holes belong in the same grammar — end of a
  4-bar phrase — so the player's answer lands where the ear expects a
  reply. [synthesis] rule: **don't fill every gap with fx; an unfilled
  hole at a phrase tail is scored FOR the soloist.** The drop-out bars
  (§4's free RMS swing) now serve double duty: arousal AND playing room.

[synthesis] batch rule going forward: every batch leaves deliberate
room — at least 2–3 beats with a sparse B section or a recurring
2–4-beat hole, logged in the README as "room for the top line."

## Engine/verify to-dos surfaced by this pass

- **Velocity accent maps only exist on the 16-grid**: velocity() softens
  e/a 16ths only when res==16 — 32-grids and New Math's 20-grid get no
  metric accents. Port the strong/weak alternation to any grid.
- **Gate hold is fixed at 140 ms**; forums cite up to 300–500 ms and say
  scale to tempo. Consider hold = one 8th note (60/bpm/2) as default.
- **Ghost-note audibility after snare trims**: −3.5 dB house + ghosts at
  ~26% risks burying the mutter. If snares still read wrong, trim
  ACCENTS harder and ghosts less (compress the range from the top),
  don't scale the whole bus again.
