# Trap Hats with Pitch Rolls — sounds like Migos "Bad and Boujee"

**Accuracy: B** — the roll-and-pitch trick is widely used. Assumes one clean, dry closed hi-hat sample.

## The Chain
Dr. Octo Rex (or NN-XT) with a hi-hat → MClass Equalizer → mixer channel

## Steps
1. Load the hi-hat into Dr. Octo Rex. Pitch 0 (you'll automate it later). High-pass filter at 800 Hz to cut low rumble. Amp envelope A 0, D 60 ms, S 0, R 40 ms.
2. MClass Equalizer: cut -6 dB shelf at 200 Hz, cut -2 dB at 4 kHz (calms harsh mids), boost +3 dB shelf at 10 kHz (sizzle).
3. Program a steady 16th-note hat pattern in the sequencer.
4. Add triplet rolls at random spots: three hits squeezed into the space of two normal 16ths.
5. Record automation on the Dr. Octo Rex transpose (pitch) control. During each roll go up 2–4 semitones, then snap back to 0.
6. Randomize velocity between 80 and 127 so it doesn't sound machine-perfect. On the Launchkey pads you can also just finger-drum the roll.

## Why this works
Hats are sparkle, not low end, so cutting lows keeps them out of the kick and snare space. The pitch climbing during a roll gives a "lift" that makes it sound expensive.

## Order of operations
EQ after the sample player so you shape the sound after its envelope.

## Reason-specific trick
Draw the pitch automation in the sequencer lane for the Dr. Octo Rex pitch control. Save the device settings as "Trap Hat.cmb".

## RE upgrade path
None required.

## Technique you just learned
Pitch automation during drum rolls. Works on snare rolls and fills too.
