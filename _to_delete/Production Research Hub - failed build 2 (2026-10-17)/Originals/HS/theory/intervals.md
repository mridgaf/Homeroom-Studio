# Intervals (two-note feelings)

An **interval** is just the gap between two notes, measured in semitones —
one semitone = one key on your Launchkey, black keys included, the smallest
possible step. Every gap has its own personality, and the classic ear-training
trick is a song that starts with that exact gap so you can sing it to
remember it.

## The feelings, small gap to big gap

- **Minor 2nd** (1 key apart): dread, creeping tension. *Jaws* theme.
- **Major 2nd** (2 keys apart): neutral motion, a simple step. *Happy Birthday* ("hap-py").
- **Minor 3rd** (3 keys apart): the classic sad building block. *Greensleeves*; *Smoke on the Water*.
- **Major 3rd** (4 keys apart): the classic happy building block. *Oh When the Saints*.
- **Perfect 4th** (5 keys apart): open, heroic, fanfare-ish. *Here Comes the Bride*; *Amazing Grace* ("A-maz").
- **Tritone** (6 keys apart): danger, evil, unresolved — nicknamed "the devil's interval." The Simpsons theme ("The Simp-"); *Maria* ("Ma-ri").
- **Perfect 5th** (7 keys apart): strong, hollow, epic. *Star Wars* theme; *Twinkle Twinkle*.
- **Minor 6th** (8 keys apart): bittersweet ache. *The Entertainer* bounce.
- **Major 6th** (9 keys apart): warmth and sweetness. NBC chime jingle; *My Bonnie*.
- **Minor 7th** (10 keys apart): soulful yearning, reaching. Star Trek (original) theme.
- **Major 7th** (11 keys apart): dreamy sting, almost-home-but-not-quite. *Take On Me* (that big opening leap).
- **Octave** (12 keys apart, same note higher): the same note but bigger — a power double. *Somewhere Over the Rainbow* ("Some-where").

## The rule of thumb

Small gaps (minor/major 2nd) = tension or intimacy. Perfect 4th/5th/octave =
strength and confidence. 3rds and 6ths carry the emotion (minor = sad, major
= sweet). The tritone = trouble, always.

## Use it on the Launchkey

Pick any key as your root, then count keys (including black ones) to find an
interval: 3 keys up = minor 3rd (instant sad hook), 4 keys up = major 3rd
(instant happy hook), 7 keys up = perfect 5th (instant power-chord strength).
Play root-then-interval back and forth for a quick 2-note melody that already
has a mood before you add anything else.
