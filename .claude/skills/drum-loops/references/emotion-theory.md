## 1. Cue-strength ranking (which knob matters most)

Ranked by measured contribution to perceived emotion (Eerola, Friberg & Bresin 2013 — factorial
synthesis study, 89% variance explained for happy/sad). Stated as a **ranking**, not raw stats —
the underlying sr² figures differ between the study's summary table and its per-emotion models, so
don't quote a single number as "mode's % of emotion."

1. **Mode** (major/minor) — the dominant valence lever. Major → happy/bright. Minor → sad/dark.
2. **Tempo** (fast/slow) — the dominant arousal lever. Faster → excited. Slower → calm/sad.
3. **Register** (pitch height) — higher → happier/brighter/more potent; lower → sadder/heavier.
4. **Dynamics** (loud/soft) — louder → powerful/angry/joyful; softer → tender/sad/fearful. Weak as a
   *static* cue but strong as a *change* (see frisson, §6).
5. **Articulation** (staccato/legato) — staccato → happy/playful; legato → sad/tender/calm.
6. **Timbre** (bright/dark) — bright → happy/energetic (or angry at high arousal); dark → sad/warm/calm.

Effects are linear and additive (no big interactions) — dial cues independently and they stack.
Mode + tempo do most of the work; treat register/dynamics/articulation/timbre as fine-tuning.

Also strong but not in the same factorial study: **melodic contour** (falling → sad/release, rising
→ tension/aspiration) and **rhythmic regularity** (firm → stable/happy, irregular → tense/agitated).

## 2. Valence/arousal quadrant recipes (Russell 1980 circumplex)

| Quadrant | Feeling | Stack |
|---|---|---|
| High arousal, positive | Happy / excited | Major, fast (120+), high register, loud, staccato, bright |
| High arousal, negative | Tense / angry / fearful | Minor or dissonant, fast, loud, harsh timbre, irregular rhythm |
| Low arousal, negative | Sad / depressed | Minor, slow (<80), low register, soft, legato, dark, falling contour |
| Low arousal, positive | Calm / tender | Major, slow, soft, legato, warm-smooth timbre, regular gentle rhythm |

Valence axis = mode + consonance/dissonance. Arousal axis = tempo + loudness + register + brightness.
Because cues are additive you can slide one axis without the other: keep minor, double the tempo,
add loud drums → sad becomes angry/driving (same valence, higher arousal).

## 3. Tempo / heart-rate anchors

- **60–80 bpm** — calming, parasympathetic; introspective/heavy in hip hop (or half-time from 120–160).
- **85–120 bpm** — neutral "comfort window" near resting HR/breathing; head-nod neutral (90–100).
- **120+ bpm** — excited, sympathetic activation; energetic/club (120–140 straight).
Treat these as soft anchors, not laws — boundaries vary by study.

## 7. Tension & release devices

- **Dominant→tonic pull** — V chord as stretched rubber band, i chord as the release.
- **Suspension** — hold a clashing note, then drop it into place (sus4→3): held breath, relief.
- **Pedal tone** — one bass note drones under changing chords; releasing it = floodgates.
- **Lament bass** — bass walks down stepwise or chromatically; centuries-old grief formula.
- **Deceptive cadence** — set up V→i, land on VI instead; the "story isn't over" rug-pull.
- **ITPRA** (Huron) — delay/deny an expected event, then confirm it slightly-violated: the "sweet"
  surprise is where musical pleasure lives (Meyer 1956; Huron 2006).

## 8. Frisson triggers (cheap and specific)

1. New voice/texture entering after a stripped-down bar — most-cited trigger.
2. Sudden dynamic swell or loud entry after quiet.
3. Unexpected/unprepared harmony (a chord you didn't see coming).
(Sloboda 1991; Panksepp 1995.)

## 9. Bass, body & repetition

- **Sub-bass presence drives felt groove**, not just low-end EQ: VLF bass raised dance movement
  ~11.8% in a live-show study (Cameron 2022); groove ratings track spectral flux in 50–100 Hz
  (Stupacher 2016). Keep 808s loud, mono, and *felt*.
- **Syncopation sweet spot**: medium syncopation maximizes groove (Witek 2014, inverted-U) — leave
  gaps, but keep the "one" recoverable. Don't go to zero (robotic) or max (loses the pulse).
- **Repetition is a feature**: mere-exposure raises liking with each repeat, up to a point
  (Margulis/Zajonc). Vary *timbre/texture* across repeats to delay wear-out — don't just loop flat.

## 10. Two frequency bands that look alike but aren't

- **30–80 Hz roughness** = amplitude-modulation/beating rate between two clashing tones (a
  perceptual "buzz" that reads as threat/aggression). This is a *rate*, not a pitch.
- **30–50 Hz sub-bass** = a literal sine-wave pitch (your 808 fundamental). Keep these separate —
  a "30 Hz" spec could mean either an LFO rate or a note, and they do different jobs.

## 11. Production devices per feeling — top 3 each

| Feeling | Devices |
|---|---|
| Nostalgic | Vinyl crackle/tape saturation + dark high shelf; pitched-up "chipmunk soul" vocal chops; borrowed-iv progression (IV–iv–I) |
| Menacing | Phrygian i–bII move; mono sub 30–50 Hz + saturation; low drone with a tritone/minor-2nd rub |
| Triumphant | I–V–vi–IV progression; perfect 4th/5th brass leaps in high register; additive layering into the hook |
| Dreamy | Imaj7–IVmaj7 loop; unison/chorus detune ±10 cents; long hall reverb tail (3–6 s, 30 ms predelay) |
| Tense | Irregular meter (7/8 over 4/4); delayed dominant→tonic resolution/suspension; detuned doubled lead (beating) |
| Melancholy | Falling minor-3rd motif / appoggiatura; exposed solo instrument, dry sparse mix; lament bass (stepwise/chromatic descent) |
| Euphoric | Fast + major + bright + staccato stack; additive build + drop-out before the hook; loud high register |
| Eerie | Microtonal/quarter-tone detuning; Shepard-tone endless riser; silence-then-hit stinger |
| Warm | min7/maj7 chords; humming wordless vocal layer; dark rolled-off timbre (soft highs) |
| Aggressive | Heavy distortion/saturation; staccato + loud + harsh timbre; dissonant stack (7#9 "Hendrix chord" or cluster) |
