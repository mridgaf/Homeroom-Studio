# Music Theory Toolkit: Eliciting Feelings (for a self-taught hip hop producer)

Plain-word glossary used throughout: **semitone** = one key on your Launchkey, black keys included (the smallest step). **Root** = the "home" note a chord/scale is named after. **Scale degree** = "which step of the ladder" (1 = home). **Flat (b)** = one key lower; **sharp (#)** = one key higher. Mood labels are consensus folklore, not physics — all of them are [folklore] by nature; the ones marked are extra-contested.

## 1. Scales & Modes = the mood palette

A **mode** is just the major scale started from a different step — same white keys, different "home", totally different feeling. Cheapest trick ever: play only white keys, choose which note is home. Semitone formula = gaps between steps (2 = whole step/skip a key, 1 = half step/next-door key).

| Mode | White keys | Semitone gaps | Mood (consensus) | Famous example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ionian (major) | C→C | 2-2-1-2-2-2-1 | happy, plain, "commercial" | Let It Be, Happy Birthday |
| Dorian | D→D | 2-1-2-2-2-1-2 | sad but cool with it; hopeful minor; funky | Get Lucky (Daft Punk), Mad World, Billie Jean verse |
| Phrygian | E→E | 1-2-2-2-1-2-2 | menacing, Spanish/flamenco, dark — the b2 (note one key above home) is the villain sound | Wherever I May Roam (Metallica); tons of drill/trap loops |
| Lydian | F→F | 2-2-2-1-2-2-1 | floating, wonder, "movie magic" — the #4 does it | The Simpsons theme, Dreams (Fleetwood Mac) [folklore on Dreams] |
| Mixolydian | G→G | 2-2-1-2-2-1-2 | happy but relaxed/bluesy, "rock & roll major" | Sweet Home Alabama, Clocks (Coldplay) |
| Aeolian (natural minor) | A→A | 2-1-2-2-1-2-2 | plain sad/serious — hip hop's default | Losing My Religion; most trap beats |
| Locrian | B→B | 1-2-2-1-2-2-2 | unstable, wrong, rarely usable whole songs | Army of Me (Björk) [folklore] |

Extras every beatmaker needs:
- **Minor pentatonic** (A-C-D-E-G; gaps 3-2-2-3-2): sad scale with the "risky" notes removed — you can't play a wrong note. Melody safety net. Ex: Smells Like Teen Spirit riff, most rap melodies.
- **Major pentatonic** (C-D-E-G-A): happy safety net. Ex: My Girl riff, Amazing Grace.
- **Blues scale** = minor pentatonic + the b5 "blue note" (A-C-D-Eb-E-G): adds grit/attitude. Ex: any blues lick, Smoke on the Water.
- **Harmonic minor** = natural minor with the 7th raised one key (A-B-C-D-E-F-G#; gap 1-3-1 at the top): dramatic, "Arabian"/old-world, villain boss music. Ex: Hit the Road Jack, Misirlou vibe, lots of dark trap.
- **Whole tone** (every gap = 2; C-D-E-F#-G#-A#): dreamlike, floating, "entering a dream sequence" in cartoons. No home feeling at all.

Sources: Musical U, Skoove, American Guitar Academy, Shockwave-Sound blog, Tunable.

## 2. Intervals = two-note feelings (with song mnemonics)

An **interval** = the distance between two notes, counted in semitones (keys). Each one has a personality. Mnemonics from the classic ear-training song charts (Musicnotes, Soundfly Flypaper, Musicca).

| Interval | Keys apart | Feeling | Sing it as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor 2nd | 1 | tension, dread, creeping | Jaws theme |
| Major 2nd | 2 | neutral step, motion | Happy Birthday ("hap-py") |
| Minor 3rd | 3 | sad building block | Greensleeves; Smoke on the Water |
| Major 3rd | 4 | happy building block | Oh When the Saints |
| Perfect 4th | 5 | open, heroic-ish, fanfare | Here Comes the Bride; Amazing Grace ("A-maz") |
| Tritone | 6 | danger, evil, unstable — the "devil's interval" | The Simpsons ("The Simp-"); Maria ("Ma-ri") |
| Perfect 5th | 7 | strong, hollow, epic | Star Wars theme; Twinkle Twinkle |
| Minor 6th | 8 | bittersweet ache | The Entertainer bounce; "fire" in We Are Young |
| Major 6th | 9 | warmth, sweetness | NBC chimes; My Bonnie |
| Minor 7th | 10 | soulful yearning | Star Trek (original) theme ("There's a place…" — Somewhere, West Side Story) |
| Major 7th | 11 | dreamy sting, almost-home | Take On Me (first big leap) |
| Octave | 12 | same note, bigger — power double | Somewhere Over the Rainbow ("Some-where") |

Rule of thumb: small gaps = tension/intimacy, perfect 4th/5th/octave = strength, 3rds/6ths = emotion (minor sad, major sweet), tritone = trouble.

## 3. Chord types = stacked feelings

A **chord** = 3+ notes at once. Numbers (7, 9) = extra notes stacked on top, each adding color.

- **Major (C-E-G)**: happy, plain, resolved.
- **Minor (C-Eb-G)**: sad, serious. Hip hop home base.
- **Diminished (C-Eb-Gb)**: panic, horror-movie sting, "something's wrong". Two stacked minor 3rds.
- **Augmented (C-E-G#)**: uneasy, sci-fi, melting. Rare but great as a passing "wobble".
- **sus2 (C-D-G) / sus4 (C-F-G)**: the 3rd (the happy/sad decider) is replaced, so the chord is "undecided" — open, airy, wants to land. Great for pads.
- **maj7 (C-E-G-B)**: dreamy, cozy, sunset. Lofi/neo-soul staple.
- **min7 (C-Eb-G-Bb)**: mellow, smoky, reflective — THE lofi chord.
- **dom7 (C-E-G-Bb)**: bluesy tension that pulls somewhere — a spring loaded to release down a 5th. All of blues lives here.
- **min9 (add a D on min7)**: lush, velvet, neo-soul luxury.
- **maj9 (add a D on maj7)**: even dreamier maj7; glassy and rich.
- **add9 (C-E-G-D)**: fresh sparkle on a plain chord, no jazz baggage.
- **7#9 "Hendrix chord" (C-E-G-Bb-D#)**: major and minor 3rd fighting in one chord = snarl, funk aggression. Purple Haze, funk stabs.

Quick Launchkey cheat: min7 = play a minor chord, add the key 2 below the root's octave. maj7 = key 1 below the octave. 9 = the key 2 above the octave.

Sources: Pickup Music, Soundfly Flypaper ("Convey 8 emotions using chords"), feelyoursound, Bandzoogle.

## 4. Progressions = feelings over time

**Roman numerals** = which scale step the chord is built on. Big letters = major chord, small = minor, b = lowered. Examples given in **A minor** (all white keys!). In A minor: i=Am, III=C, iv=Dm, v/V=Em/E, VI=F, VII=G.

- **Sad, accepting**: i–VI–III–VII → Am–F–C–G. The "sad anthem" loop (Zombie, Numb). Melancholy that still moves forward.
- **Sad, sinking**: i–iv–VI–V → Am–Dm–F–E. The E (major V, borrowed from harmonic minor) is a knife-twist of drama.
- **Uplifting**: I–V–vi–IV → in C: C–G–Am–F. THE pop "triumph" loop (hundreds of hits). Relative-major cousin of the sad loop above.
- **Nostalgic #1 (borrowed iv)**: IV–iv–I → in C: F–Fm–C. The Fm is "borrowed" from the minor world — instant wistful sigh (Radiohead, Beatles trick).
- **Nostalgic #2 (jazz handshake)**: ii–V–I → in C: Dm7–G7–Cmaj7. Smooth "coming home" motion; backbone of jazz/soul/lofi (Native Instruments blog, Chordoo).
- **Epic**: i–VII–VI(–VII) → Am–G–F–G. Marching descent, trailer music, stadium minor.
- **Dark/menacing (Phrygian move)**: i–bII → Am–Bb. Home chord to the chord one key up = pure menace. Drill/horrorcore staple.
- **Dreamy**: Imaj7–IVmaj7 loop → Cmaj7–Fmaj7 (or in A minor world: Fmaj7–Gmaj7 colors). Two floaty chords forever = lofi heaven.
- **Gospel/church**: I–I7–IV (the I7 is a "secondary dominant" — a spring pointing at IV), plus walk-ups like IV–#IVdim–I/V and the "backdoor" bVII7–I. Consensus recipe: dominant 7ths everywhere + bass walking between chords.
- **Hip hop / neo-soul loops**: two-chord vamps rule — i–iv7 (Am–Dm7), i–VI (Am–F), ii7–V7 loop that never resolves (Dm7–G7 forever = suspended chill), i–v7 (Am–Em7). Trap favorites: i–VI–III–VII and i–VI–iv–V (Metro Boomin-style dark loops — Unison, Chordoo, Mixed In Key). The loop IS the mood; rappers need harmony that doesn't demand attention.

## 5. Tension & release = the engine of feeling

Music works like breathing: tighten, then let go.
- **Dominant → tonic pull**: the V chord (E7 in A minor) is a stretched rubber band; the i chord is letting it snap home. The leading tone (G#, one key below home) is the stretch.
- **Suspension**: hold a note from the last chord that clashes with the new one, then drop it into place (sus4 → 3). Feels like a held breath, then relief.
- **Pedal tone**: keep ONE bass note droning while chords change above it. Builds pressure like standing water; releasing the pedal = floodgates.
- **Lament bass**: bass walks DOWN step by step (A–G–F–E), or chromatically every key (A–G#–G–F#–F–E). Centuries-old grief formula — Dido's Lament, Hit the Road Jack, Hotel California verse (Wikipedia/Gettysburg music theory).
- **Deceptive cadence ("rug pull")**: set up V like you're going home to i… land on VI instead (E7 → F). Listener's stomach drops; instant "story isn't over" feeling.
- Practical loop trick: make bar 4 of your loop the tension bar (V chord, sus, or riser) so the loop restart feels like release.

## 6. Bass & 808 note choice

The bass note is the chord's anchor — whatever's lowest, the ear treats as "what this chord IS."
- **Root in the bass** = solid, confident, done. Default: put the 808 on the chord's root (every trap guide agrees — Melodics, Production Music Live).
- **Fifth in the bass** = strong but restless, "on the way somewhere". Good on beat 3 or as a slide target.
- **Octave jumps** = energy without changing harmony. Root-octave bounce = instant drive.
- **Third in the bass** = wobbly/unstable, because the ear wants the root under a chord; the 3rd underneath makes the chord feel tilted, mid-sentence. Use deliberately for walking motion, not as home.
- **808 rule**: chord roots on strong beats; passing notes (5th, b7, slides) only between them, resolving to the next root. And tune the 808 to the key — an off-key 808 quietly ruins everything (KyuBeats, RouteNote).

## 7. Rhythm & feel

- **Halftime feels heavy** because the snare lands half as often (beat 3 instead of 2&4) — the ear counts fewer "footsteps", so each one feels like a giant's. Same BPM, double the perceived weight.
- **Double-time feels frantic**: snares twice as often = little feet running.
- **Syncopation** = accenting the "wrong" (off) beats. The brain expects the grid; hitting between gridlines creates tiny surprises = groove.
- **Anticipation** = playing a note slightly BEFORE the beat it belongs to (the Cuban "anticipated bass"). Feels eager, pulls the track forward.
- **Tresillo** = 3+3+2: divide 8 eighth-notes as long-long-short (hits on 1, the "and" of 2, and 4). A "fake triplet" that leans forward — the engine under dembow, trap hats, dancehall, and countless rap flows (Open Music Theory, Wikipedia, The FADER).
- Swing/late snares = laid-back drunk feel (Dilla); rushed hats = anxious energy. Milliseconds are emotions.

## 8. Hip hop specifics

- **Why rap lives in minor/dorian/phrygian**: rap's affect is serious — struggle, menace, cool. Minor = serious by default; dorian adds "cool with it" swagger (the brighter 6th); phrygian's b2 adds threat. Major keys read as "cheerful", which fights most rap vocals. Also melodic-minimalism: minor pentatonic loops leave maximal space for the voice. (Consensus across Native Instruments, Chordoo, eMastered.) [folklore, but universal folklore]
- **Sample-flip theory — why chopping changes harmony**: a chord's meaning comes from its context (what's before/after, and the bass under it). Chop a sample and (1) you reorder the chords = new story from the same words; (2) you loop a chord that was "passing" = it becomes the new home; (3) you put YOUR 808 under it = re-harmonizing: the same Dm7 slice over an F bass becomes an Fmaj9 — dreamier, no new notes needed (BeatKey, LANDR, Slime Green Beats). Pitching the whole chop up/down also relocates it to a new key with new grit (chipmunk-soul = pitch-up artifacts as emotion).
- **Common trap keys** [folklore + partial stats]: producers gravitate to **C# minor and F minor** — folklore says C# minor became "the trap key" partly because 808s slap in that register and stock Fruity Loops/phone-keyboard habits spread it. One published tally of Billboard's top trap hits found ~60% in F or G (incl. minors) (Celebritycolumn); other guides name Em, Bm, Fm as favorites (Songen, Unison). Real rule: pick the key where your specific 808 sample's sweet spot sits — usually roots between E1 and G1.
- **The 2-bar loop is the song**: trap harmony is usually 1–4 chords looped; emotion comes from subtraction (muting the chords for a bar), octave lifts, and drum switch-ups, not chord changes.
