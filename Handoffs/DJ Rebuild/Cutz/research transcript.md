## Drums, swing, grid
- Gear: SP-12 then Alesis HR-16 (named "HR-11" in Wax Poetics) then MPC60; Step in the Arena was still Alesis; he "didn't really start using the MPC until Daily Operation" (Wax Poetics, Premier himself). MPC60 triggers drums, S950 holds samples, 60 seconds max (Sound On Sound). RouteNote says SP1200 early, then the Akai rig by Hard to Earn. [CONFIRMED that the MPC60/S950 rig is the mid-90s rig; the SP-12/SP1200 dates disagree: UNSURE]
- Drums: "sometimes drum loops and sometimes individual hits, combining them" (Wax Poetics) [UNSURE one source]. N.Y. State of Mind drums are a nearly unedited drum passage from Kool & the Gang "N.T.", pitched down (Mode Audio; Wikipedia agrees on the sample) [CONFIRMED]. Mass Appeal drums are a cut-up loop (Wikipedia, Mode Audio); a "moderately syncopated" kick and "shuffling" hat (Beattips) [UNSURE one source]. Marley Marl taught him to use kick and snare from the record, not the whole break (Soul-Sides, Premier) [CONFIRMED with Wax Poetics]. RouteNote: "tight, trimmed one-shot" kicks, hats, snares from dusty breaks [UNSURE, RouteNote leans on 1998-2003].
- Feel: he turns off the 16th notes and plays the MPC live "so it sounds like live drumming... a little bounce" (Sound On Sound, Premier quoted in DJ Premium's old note, and my SoS read: "a little bounce", "slightly behind-the-beat"). Mode Audio: "laid-back, heavily swung drums" on Mass Appeal and NY State [CONFIRMED loose and behind the beat].
- **Swing %:** only RouteNote gives a number (51-54) and it is a blog estimate; Mode Audio says "heavily swung". The two disagree on how much. [UNSURE] 51-54 stays; NOT FOUND from Premier or an engineer. Measuring audio in Code is the real fix.
- Hat grid, ghost snares, snare offset in ms: NOT FOUND.

## Samples and instruments, ranked
1. **Jazz loops chopped small** [CONFIRMED]: he chose jazz because it had less vocal "so you could take more pieces and chop it up" (Wax Poetics); "no one really chopped those" (RBMA); chops isolate phrases and leave gaps (RouteNote); deliberately obscures samples (SoS). Learned filtering from Large Professor and chopping from the clipped-drum trend (Soul-Sides).
2. **Guitar**: Vic Juris "Horizon Drive" (Mass Appeal), Donald Byrd "Flight Time" (NY State) [CONFIRMED, 2 tracks, 2 sources each].
3. **Piano / vibes-piano**: Joe Chambers "Mind Rain" (NY State: Wikipedia, Mode Audio); a solo piano loop on "DJ Premier in Deep Concentration" (Wax Poetics) [CONFIRMED].
4. **Organ**: Reuben Wilson "We're in Love" (Memory Lane) and Lee Erwin organ (Represent), both on the Illmatic page. I did NOT confirm in this session that Premier made those two tracks. [UNSURE, left out of the line]
5. **Horns**: Maceo "Parrty" horn whistle cut by hand on a Life After Death track (Substack, 1997); Kool & Gang "N.T." sax is Large Professor's (Wikipedia). Stabs "everybody started getting into that" came AFTER the label asked for jazz (RBMA). So horn stabs are NOT the mid-90s sound. [CONFIRMED: this is the era boundary]
6. **Keys he plays**: "After Step in the Arena he gained access to better gear and learned keys" (Substack); keyboards MIDI'd to the MPC (SoS); RouteNote lists spacey bleeps and atmospheric pads [UNSURE].
7. **Bass**: "deep bass" (SoS); he often follows the sample's bass line, "I'm playing the same notes verbatim" (SoS) [CONFIRMED by SoS only; "deep" word is a misfire risk]. Mind Rain has a double bass (Wikipedia, Illmatic). No kick-locked bass claim anywhere.
8. **Scratches** [CONFIRMED]: cuts vocal samples into new hooks (Wikipedia, RouteNote, Substack, Complex). Unbelievable and NY State both have scratched hooks. Where in the bar/loop (bars 4 and 8): NOT FOUND, carried over.
9. **Birdsong/bells**: sci-fi bleeps and birdsong (RouteNote, 1999 Nas Is Like); bells in the MC's Act intro (Complex, 1995) [UNSURE].

## Key and chords
- Keys found: A#/Bb minor-ish x4 (Mass Appeal, Take It Personal, Come Clean, MC's), F#/Gb x2 (NY State, Unbelievable), F, D, C#. Mode NOT FOUND per track; jazz sources lean minor; Jay-Z asked Premier for "minor keys" for D'Evils 1996 (Complex).
- Premier builds a two-bar melody that repeats (Last.fm via search, UNSURE). Arrangements: two- or four-bar loops (RouteNote). No source for arpeggios.

- Pro Tools, Control 24, NS-10s (RouteNote) are the 1998-2003 setup, not this era. Clean mix, gated space, dust level: NOT FOUND for 1992-96. Static left in on a 1996 track (RBMA "Question Remains") [UNSURE, one source].

## Quotes (under 15 words)
- Premier: "I was really into jazz samples because no one really capped those" (RBMA).
- Premier: "It's all in how you bounce them over the samples" (Wax Poetics).
- Premier: "Whenever I produce, I do it with a DJ mentality" (Substack).
- Premier: "I'm playing the same notes verbatim" (Sound On Sound).
- Premier on Mass Appeal: "I made the background melody real simplistic" (Wikipedia).

## Who worked with him
- **Guru** (Gang Starr): writes to Premier's beats from a title list (Soul-Sides).
- **Nas**: wrote and recorded N.Y. State of Mind verse "in one take" over the beat (Complex, Wikipedia).
- **Large Professor** taught him filtering; **Marley Marl** the kick/snare trick (Soul-Sides, Premier's own account).
- **Jeru the Damaja**: Come Clean kept raw, no keys (Complex).
- **KRS-One / Notorious B.I.G. / Jay-Z**: sessions told in Complex; none speak about the sound in the sources I could open.
- Pete Rock and Aguilera praise him (DJBooth/Wikipedia) but not about this era's sound. Rolling Stone "15 Songs" is behind a paywall.
