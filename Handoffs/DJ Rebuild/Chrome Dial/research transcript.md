## Who made what
- Timbaland made the beats; Missy Elliott co-wrote/co-produced most; Static Major co-wrote Pony, Are You That Somebody, Try Again (Wikipedia, several pages) [CONFIRMED].
- Engineer Jimmy Douglass tracked and mixed Pony, The Bachelor, Are You That Somebody, Try Again (Wikipedia credits; Fader; Revolt) [CONFIRMED].
- Supa Dupa Fly recorded at Master Sound, Virginia Beach (Wikipedia; RBMA) [CONFIRMED].

# Part 2: drums, silence, swing

## Silence and space [CONFIRMED]
- Sneakz & Beatz: "silence used as an instrument" is shared by One in a Million, The Bachelor, Supa Dupa Fly. Coos or crickets stand where a hi-hat would.
- U92 / Talmage Garn: "Negative space becomes groove" (quoted). Gaps left instead of filling bars.
- Talkhouse (Schatz on the Timbaland MasterClass): Timbaland keeps tracks minimal for Missy, "an instrument herself"; praises "the silences in between".
- Revolt on Get Ur Freak On: sparse drums, hits "land in unexpected places".
- Ethan Hein: tracks have silence between notes, bone-dry, no reverb or delay [UNSURE: his general claim, not tied to this era].

## Kick, snare, claps, hats
- Kick lands in unexpected places / stuttered placements (Sneakz on OIAM and The Rain; U92) [CONFIRMED].
- Douglass on the kick: he ignored tape-level warnings and "Mother Nature did compression" (Fader). So the famous kick was overloaded on purpose [UNSURE: one source, one engineer]. This leans against pure "completely clean" for the kick.
- Snare/claps: "boom bap drums" with hand claps, snares, hi-hats on If Your Girl Only Knew; double-time hand claps on It's Hot; "synthetic hand claps and mouth percussion" on We Need a Resolution; "programmed snares, kicks and shakers" on Try Again (Complex x2, Wikipedia) [CONFIRMED that claps and snares carry the backbeat].
- Hats: CONFLICT. Sneakz: crickets/coos replace hats. Forum/U92: OIAM hats "stutter" and return doubled. Complex: "skittering hi-hats" (Nigga What, Nigga Who), "hi-hats" on IYGOK. So hats exist on some tracks and are absent on others [UNSURE].
- Beatbox: drum figures sampled from the mouth (Sneakz); Timbaland: "I fill up the space with my mouth" (Talkhouse); beatboxing on Make It Hot, Are You That Somebody (Complex) [CONFIRMED].
- Drum and bass / UK breakbeat syncopation behind OIAM and The Rain (Sneakz; AllMusic "slowed-down drum'n'bass breakbeats", Wikipedia SDF) [CONFIRMED].

## Swing
- NOT FOUND from Timbaland or an engineer. One blog (Garn, U92) estimates 58-62%, cites a swing graph the page does not show [UNSURE]. iZotope: syncopation alone sits on a straight grid. Current lanes are 50 (straight).
- The ASR-10 sequencer is 96 PPQ, 16 track (Wikipedia). A groove-template maker sells ASR-10 1/16 and triplet quantize files, "a special feel" (mixonline) [UNSURE].

# Part 3: gear, instruments, bass, harmony

## Gear
- Ensoniq ASR-10 sampler/keyboard, 16-bit, 29.76/44.1 kHz (GearNews; Wikipedia ASR-10; RBMA). Lenny Holmes (guitarist): Timbaland "chopped up the guitar note and put it on a key" in it (RBMA) [CONFIRMED].
- Talkhouse/Schatz: sounds "chopped, and mapped across the keys", car screech on alternating beats in Pony [CONFIRMED with RBMA].
- Timbaland himself (Drink Champs via Revolt): played parts by ear, no computers or clicks in late-90s sessions; a Casio sampled one second for his first beats.
- Drums labeled by number, composed on an unlabeled grid (Talkhouse): so no fixed kit.
- Korg Triton came 1999 (GearNews, general, not tied to a track) [UNSURE]. Later Pro Tools (Holmes).
- Douglass: analog 24-track locked to digital 48-track; Timbaland's parts on the analog tracks (Revolt) [CONFIRMED].

## Instruments, ranked by sources
1. Exotic plucked/wind leads: tumbi + tabla (Wikipedia, Hein, Revolt, Complex GUFO); Egyptian-sample flute loop (Big Pimpin', Wikipedia); sitar and synthetic sitar (Try Again, Phat Rabbit); Asian strings and squelching synth (You Owe Me); koto (Hein). [CONFIRMED] 4+ sources.
2. Guitars: staccato guitars (Are You That Somebody, Wikipedia + Complex), jazz guitars (Trippin'), wah-wah (It's Hot), picked guitar (Hey Papi), gospel-style guitar (So Anxious), Holmes's guitar chopped into the ASR-10. [CONFIRMED]
3. Synths: squelching/skittering/droning synth leads (Try Again, Trippin', Pony talkbox-like, Get Ur Freak On droning synths). [CONFIRMED]
4. Organ and keys: "heavy keyboard and organ" (IYGOK, Wikipedia); piano (So Anxious); Latin-sounding keys (Make It Hot). [UNSURE] each one track.
5. Strings and horns: menacing horns and strings (Sock It 2 Me), plucked strings (You Won't See Me Tonight), strings as bass line (Is That Your Bitch). [UNSURE] Complex only.
6. Bells, crickets, blips: bells and blippy synths (Beats 4 da Streets, Wikipedia); cricket-like sounds (OIAM, The Rain).
7. Found sounds as rhythm: baby cry/laugh (Are You That Somebody; Timbaland says from a sound-effects record), car screeches, a horse's whinny, storm and cricket noise (Pony, The Rain). [CONFIRMED]

## Bass [CONFIRMED that it is rubbery/slappy, sources disagree on exact sound]
- "slap bass" and "thumping bassline" (IYGOK, Complex/Wikipedia); "snaky, distorted bass line" (Try Again); "squelching talkbox" and a processed vocal as the low end (Pony: Billboard via Wikipedia; Sneakz); "rubbery funk" (The Rain); "subterranean bass" later (2003).

## Key and harmony
- Minor keys dominate in the tools above: G#m, C#m, Bm/F#m, Em, Am. Phrygian only named for Get Ur Freak On (Hein) [UNSURE for the rest]. No chord vamps published. NOT FOUND.

# Part 4: people who worked with him
- Jimmy Douglass (engineer): "picks crazy samples and he knows how to put them together" (Fader); kick overload quote above (Fader).
- Missy Elliott (co-producer), on Get Ur Freak On: he hit something on the keyboard and "that's it right there" (Revolt, paraphrase of her recall).
- Lenny Holmes (guitarist) and Melvin Barcliff (friend/engineer-era), RBMA: guitar chopped onto a key; Pony came from "just playing around with some sounds".
- Magoo (Timbaland & Magoo): Virginia was "not a Hip Hop area"; Timbaland already ahead of peers (youknowigotsoul).
- Timbaland himself: "I took that rhythm and I listened back to it" (Oompa Loompa, Okayplayer); "I fill up the space with my mouth" (Talkhouse paraphrase).
