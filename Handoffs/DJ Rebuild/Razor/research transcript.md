## What the sources say
- Sonic collages from classic soul samples and martial-arts movie clips; "raw, underground sound" (Wikipedia, Enter the Wu-Tang) [CONFIRMED]. "Dirty quality": drums with more bass, hard-hitting not crisp, "eerie, almost haunting type of echo" (Wikipedia) [CONFIRMED].
- "Rumble jumble of drumbeats, peppered with occasional piano plunking" (Entertainment Weekly via Wikipedia) [CONFIRMED with the C.R.E.A.M. piano riff].
- Gear: SP-1200 (bought 1988), then Ensoniq ASR-10 ("my top instrument... I made so many of my Wu-Tang songs on that keyboard" RZA), MPC only from '96-'97; "combined the MPC with the ASR-10 and that's how I made 'Triumph'" (RZA, SynthHistory) [CONFIRMED]. Deliberate grit from the SP-1200's low sampling rate, and 33 rpm records sampled at 45 and replayed slower (Sweetwater) [CONFIRMED].
- Soul samples chopped, sped up or slowed down (Wikipedia) [CONFIRMED].
- Not re-found: "widest jitter on the roster", "heaviest vinyl bed and a warp of wow", "off-kilter muddy boom kicks".
