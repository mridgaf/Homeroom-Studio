# Acid Rap Bright: research transcript, 2026-10-23 (Part 1 of 3: what it is and the tracks)

Fresh research for this genre only. Era/lineage: Chance the Rapper's *Acid Rap* (April 30, 2013, free mixtape, Chicago) and the Chicago circle around it (Peter CottonTale, Nate Fox, Cam O'bi, Stefan Ponce, Nico Segal / Donnie Trumpet). The Social Experiment's *Surf* (2015) is the same circle but is NOT researched here.
Tags: [CONFIRMED] = 2+ sources or the person themselves. [UNSURE] = one secondary source or sources disagree. NOT FOUND = nobody published it.
This is a GENRE, not a person: the quality bar for people is "2+ practitioners". Practitioners with their own words: Peter CottonTale, Nate Fox, Stefan Ponce, Cam O'bi, Ludwig Goransson, JP Floyd, Nico Segal, Elton Chueng (mixing). Eight.

## Why this is a different sound from Acid Rap Detroit
Detroit = Esham 1989, dark horrorcore, hiss and dust. Bright = Chicago 2013, live horns and keys, clean. Nothing shared: the config and this note keep them apart.

# Part 2 of 3: who made it, instruments, drums

## Who did what (people's own words)
- Stefan Ponce: Chance "wanted to have an acid-jazz inspired sound for this project". On Good Ass Intro: "I only ever did the juke-style drums for this song." Live: "I'd do the drums and Peter would play the keys." [CONFIRMED: also Wikipedia's "acid jazz" genre tag and Chance's own "heavy Acid Jazz base" quote]
- Peter CottonTale (Wilkins): "wanted me to flip and add chords on it" (the Common/John Legend sample). He played keys on the Cocoa Butter Kisses remake. Developed the "gospel, soul, and hip-hop" vocal arrangements with Lili K (Complex oral history, summarised by the fetch tool; exact quote not re-read this session [UNSURE on wording]).
- Cam O'bi: "Peter was on keys and I was on my laptop." Programmed the drums in FL Studio (Wikipedia, Cocoa Butter Kisses).
- Nate Fox: chopped samples "really simply". Willie Hutch sample is "just an eight-bar loop". Juice and Favorite Song were made the same day (oral history).
- Ludwig Goransson: Chance "wanted guitar and organ" for Interlude. Started from a piano intro on his iPhone; built in Ableton.
- JP Floyd: "Peter had a vision and he just needed the horn players." Played trombone on Good Ass Intro. Nico Segal: trumpet on the Outro (Chance had him interpolate "Faithful").
- Elton Chueng (mixed and mastered the whole tape): his first full-length mix. Drums on NaNa were a loop he wanted to "smack". Mixed Good Ass Intro more than ten times because of its many layers.
- Vic Mensa on Cam O'bi: he "brought those drums and that real natural, good-feeling sound" (Andscape). Mensa also called the Cocoa Butter Kisses beat "bossa nova" (oral history summary) [UNSURE, one source for bossa].
- Chance (Wikipedia, via Spin interview): "Peter Cottontale is a really sick jazz pianist." Also: the new music he listened to "has got a really heavy Acid Jazz base".

## Instruments, ranked by how many sources name them
1. Horns: trombone (JP Floyd), trumpet (Nico Segal), more horn players brought by CottonTale; DJBooth: Good Ass Intro "uplifted by horn riffs"; Complex 10-year interview: "prominent horns". [CONFIRMED, 4 sources]
2. Keys / piano: CottonTale on keys (Cam O'bi), "sick jazz pianist" (Chance), "thoughtfully laid keys" (Andscape), Goransson's piano intro. [CONFIRMED]. "Fender Rhodes" appears only in a 2020 CottonTale solo-work video, NOT Acid Rap: do not claim Rhodes.
3. Organ: Goransson (guitar and organ); AllMusic via Wikipedia on Cocoa Butter Kisses: "church organ swells and synthesizer swirls". [CONFIRMED, 2 sources]
4. Guitar: Goransson (Interlude); Betty Wright sample has "tropical guitar" (Wikipedia). [CONFIRMED the record has it; nobody says how often]
5. Soul/jazz sample loops: Hathaway, Willie Hutch, Betty Wright, Grusin, Slum Village, Common/Legend. [CONFIRMED]. The loops are chopped simply (Fox).
6. Synth: "synthesizer swirls" (AllMusic, one source). [UNSURE]
7. Sung choir-style vocal stacks: six vocalists on Good Ass Intro (DJBooth), Lili K, BJ, Kiara Lanier. [CONFIRMED] but the engine's choir pool is 2 samples.
8. Not named by anyone: strings, bells, pads, plucks, woodwinds-as-sample-group. (Brass counts as "horns" in the engine; "wood" in the engine is a different group.)

# Part 3 of 3: mix, harmony, bass, gaps, current vs research

## Mix and sound
- Chance's vocals are unprocessed, no Auto-Tune (DJBooth). Drum sound "honest" (Chueng, per the 2026-09-24 note) [UNSURE: that quote came from the earlier session, not re-read]. So: clean. [CONFIRMED enough for "no dust": nothing in the research asks for dirt.]
- Space of silence: Pusha Man to Paranoia, a deliberate near-30-second gap; Fox says James Blake's use of space was an influence. Song-level, the engine can't do it.
- Horns tracked live at Soundscape; the tape looped end to start (Chueng). Song-level, not buildable.

## Key and harmony
- Keys on songbpm: mixed (F#, C#, A...); major/minor NOT published by that site. The earlier 2026-09-24 note counted 4 major / 4 minor [UNSURE]. So the research does not support "major-key" as the line's first word. The mode weights stay in the config (carried over); only the claim leaves the line.
- Jazz/gospel harmony: "acid jazz" is [CONFIRMED] by Ponce, Chance and Wikipedia. Chord names/progressions: NOT FOUND for any track. Gospel: CottonTale and Lili K's vocal arrangements; "Sunday Candy carries over Acid Rap's gospel and soul" (a review, secondary [UNSURE]). In the engine, gospel can only show up as a progression (gospel_walkup) and a choir-like voice.
