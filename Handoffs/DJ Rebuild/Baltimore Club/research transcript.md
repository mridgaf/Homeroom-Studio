# Baltimore Club: research transcript, 2026-10-23 (Part 1 of 1)

Fresh research for this genre only. Era: the classic Baltimore club run, about 1991-2008 (Unruly Records, Frank Ski, Scottie B, DJ Technics, KW Griff, DJ Class, Rod Lee). The later "shake-off" sound (K-Spin, Blaqstarr, 132-150 BPM, gunshots) is noted but is NOT the target. Jersey/Philly club are different genres and not touched.

## Drums
- Kick: "enormous, muffled kick drums" and "giant 808 kicks" (RBMA, as described in the fetch) [CONFIRMED with Booman: a "bass kick" that is an 808, EQ'd "a million times"]. RA: "blown-out kicks, rattling subs". Baltimore Sun, KW Griff: the test was whether "the kick drums kicked real hard".
- Kick figure: No Tags says "five-beat kick pattern". The Vice article (BLOCKED, only a search summary) reportedly puts kicks evenly on 1, 2, 3, one slightly after 4, and a "thump" just before 1: that is also five. RA separately notes a delayed 4th kick on Break It Down. Five hits per bar: [CONFIRMED] (No Tags + RA + Vice summary, the last secondhand). Exact grid: NOT FOUND (nobody transcribed it).
- Claps/snare: "breakbeats and handclaps" (Village Voice) [UNSURE, one source]. "Syncopated snares" and "rapid-fire hi-hat rolls" (Sound Atlas, a generic summary) [UNSURE]. Clap on 2 and 4 + Think break (John's Guide, carried from the 2026-09-24 note, not re-opened) [UNSURE]. The current engine puts claps on the "a" of 2 and "&" of 3: NOT FOUND in any source.
- Hats: NOT FOUND in detail.
- Breaks: the Think break (Lyn Collins) and the Sing Sing break (Gaz, 1978, shuffling shakers) are the backbone [CONFIRMED: RBMA, Wikipedia, SPIN, Sun, No Tags, Futureproof]. Booman: Sing Sing "like the glue". Scottie B tells the idea: play a Think break and break it up with Sing Sing under it. Amen break on one Unruly record (RA).
- Rod Lee's palette: SPIN says the drum breaks have not changed since "It Takes Two" (Rob Base) and the tracks are "a few shouted chants over a drum machine stuck on repeat".

## Instruments and samples ranked
1. Sampled breaks (Think, Sing Sing, Amen), chopped [CONFIRMED]
2. Vocal chants and looped snippets, call-and-response [CONFIRMED: Wikipedia, RBMA, SPIN, Sun, K-Spin, No Tags]
3. Horns: "horns went with everything" after Tear Da Club Up (RBMA, as relayed); RA says its horns work on big speakers [CONFIRMED, two sources]
4. 808 sub/bass [CONFIRMED]
5. "Tinny synths", rising arpeggio under an Amen break (RA), "compressed, flat chords" on Whatzup (RA) [UNSURE, one source]
6. Minor-key piano from a sampled rap hook (SPIN) [UNSURE, one source]
7. Gunshots, bangs, Lil Jon "what/hey/yeah" (K-Spin, No Tags): later shake-off era, not the target.
Guitar, organ, strings, bells, pads, choir, wood, pluck: NOT FOUND in any source.

## Mix and arrangement
"Distorted and loud and crunchy" (Technics, RBMA); Mighty Mark "rawness or grit, some distortion and grime" (RBMA, from old note). Booman: "loud, obnoxious, and energetic" (Sun). Levels "out of control" at the pressing plant. Gear: Ensoniq ASR-10, SP-1200, EPS 16+, Tascam four-track, DAT (RBMA, Sun, Futureproof) [CONFIRMED]. Reverb: NOT FOUND (the engine's "dry" stays). Arrangement: tracks are "extremely simple and repetitive", switched every couple of minutes in mixes (Village Voice).
