Read: the core pocket is 92-96, home 95. The one slow outlier is 82. Wikipedia's genre page says "typically set at between 90 and 100 BPM" (one secondary source, but it agrees with the table above). The 105-106 tracks in the old note (Tonite, Fantastic Voyage) were not re-verified this session: [UNSURE].
Quik's songs are not all slow: tunebat lists "Can I Eat It?" at 105 and getsongbpm lists "Get At Me" at 109. Those are outside the G-funk pocket.

## Who made what (all [CONFIRMED] unless marked)
- Warren G made Regulate on an Akai MPC60, Technics 1200s and a Numark mixer in a North Long Beach apartment (Wikipedia Regulate, citing Billboard, Spin, Rolling Stone).
- Regulate = a four-bar loop from Michael McDonald's "I Keep Forgettin'" (Billboard; Wikipedia). Also Bob James "Sign of the Times", whistled by Warren G himself.
- The engineer Greg Geitzenauer added keyboards with a string sound from a Yamaha SY77, a riff "in the style of a Hammond organ at the end of every fourth bar", and a Minimoog-type sound for the movie-dialogue intro (Wikipedia Regulate) [UNSURE: one secondary source for the gear names].

# Part 2 of 4: tempo, swing, drums

## Tempo
- 90-100 BPM (Wikipedia G-funk). 92-96 recommended (note.com tutorial). Table above: 92-96 with an 82 outlier. [CONFIRMED] for 92-96.

## Swing
- NOT FOUND from any producer. One tutorial (note.com) says "about 54-58%" light swing. [UNSURE]. The current 56 sits inside that. It stays as "your pick".

## Snare
- "heavy use of the snare drum" (Wikipedia G-funk). Tutorial: snare fixed on 2 and 4, clap layered on it (note.com). [CONFIRMED] for "heavy snare", [UNSURE] for the clap layer (one source).
- Hutch (Above the Law): the sound "had to have a slap that was hardcore" so it wasn't only smooth (Okayplayer). Quik wants "the brightest snares or claps" (The Fader). [CONFIRMED] snare/clap is bright and hard.

## Kick and hats
- Kick: Quik wants "the funkiest, heaviest drums" (The Fader). No kick pattern or tuning published. NOT FOUND.
- Hats: tutorial says steady eighths with space, downbeats louder, an open hat on an offbeat every two bars (note.com). Nobody who worked on the records says it. [UNSURE], one source.
- Percussion: tambourine or shaker is "a common technique" in 90s West Coast tracks (note.com). [UNSURE], one source.

## Drum machines and mix
- Warren G: MPC60 (Wikipedia Regulate; his own post says his MPC was used on The Chronic, Regulate and The G Funk Era [UNSURE: social post, not opened]).
- Quik: SP-1200 for his early records (POW), LinnDrum "still hot to me", plus DMX, Kraftwerk and YMO rhythms as models (The Fader).
- Feel: "laid-back", weight on the back of the beat (note.com, Wikipedia). Tutorial says push the bass 10-20 ticks behind. NOT a producer quote [UNSURE].

# Part 3 of 4: instruments, bass, harmony

## Instruments, ranked by how many sources name them
1. High synth lead, portamento saw wave, the "whistle" (Wikipedia G-funk; note.com; Diverse Mentality: "worm sounds"; Hutch: "siren sounds, crazy keyboards"). [CONFIRMED]. The engine has no whistle lead and cannot make one (no-machine-tones rule). Gap.
2. Deep bass, played live or on a synth (Wikipedia: "a deep bass"; Krims: "heavy on bass and keyboards"; Hutch: "live bass"; Yams: "thick, rolling basslines"; Wikipedia Regulate: "rippling bass"). [CONFIRMED].
3. Keyboards: electric piano, Wurlitzer, Rhodes. Wikipedia: "electric piano". POW: Quik bought a Wurlitzer for Safe + Sound. [CONFIRMED].
4. Organ: Hammond-style riff every fourth bar in Regulate (Wikipedia Regulate) [UNSURE, one source].
5. Strings/pad: SY77 string sound in Regulate (same source) [UNSURE].
6. Funk guitar: Quik with guitarist Robert Bacon (POW); "funk-inspired guitar riffs" (Yams). [CONFIRMED] it exists in the genre, but nobody says how often.
7. Talkbox: Roger Troutman taught Quik (Wikipedia); Yams, note.com list it. [CONFIRMED]. No talkbox sample in his library. Gap.
8. Background female vocals (Wikipedia). [UNSURE]. No such samples. Gap.
9. Horn stabs: only in Quik's early records (POW: "the horn stabs sharp"). One source, one era. Not carried into the line.
10. Whistling: Warren G whistled a riff himself on Regulate (Billboard) [CONFIRMED, one track].

## Sampling vs playing
- "minimal (sometimes no) sampling", live instrumentation (Krims, quoted in Wikipedia). Dre used live players to replay samples (Wikipedia; note.com). Hutch: "taking P-Funk and slowing it down, adding live bass" (Diverse Mentality). [CONFIRMED]. Regulate itself is a sampled four-bar loop, so both ways exist.

## Harmony
- Krims: "often highly conventional harmonic progressions" (Wikipedia). Quik: "jazz chords" and vocal harmonies in his later records (Yams). POW: Quik's progressions "always felt preordained". These disagree a little: simple loops at the core (Regulate is a four-bar loop), jazzier chords in Quik's later records. [UNSURE] on exact chords.
- Hutch's background: "jazz-blues family", Isaac Hayes, Parliament-Funkadelic (Okayplayer).
- Regulate chords from a user tab: Em7/G, Bm7/F#, Bm7/E... (Ultimate Guitar) [UNSURE, user transcription].
- Keys across the tracks above: B minor, E minor, F# minor, F minor, G major. Mostly minor, some major or mixolydian. [UNSURE, tunebat's machine guesses].

# Part 4 of 4: quotes, who carries it, comparison, sources

## Quotes (under 15 words)
- Quik: "the funkiest, heaviest drums; the brightest snares or claps" (The Fader, 2014).
- Hutch (Above the Law): "it had to have a slap that was hardcore" (Okayplayer).
- Hutch: "I started putting more melody into hardcore Hip-Hop" (Ambrosia for Heads).
- Hutch: "slowed it down a bit" plus "siren sounds, crazy keyboards" (Ambrosia for Heads).
- Warren G: the McDonald record "brought back feelings" of his parents' house (Billboard). Not a sound quote.
- Krims: "live instrumentation, heavy on bass and keyboards, with minimal (sometimes no) sampling" (via Wikipedia).

## Who carries it
Bass and keys carry it. Drums are steady and hard but are not the story. The lead melody is a high synth.
