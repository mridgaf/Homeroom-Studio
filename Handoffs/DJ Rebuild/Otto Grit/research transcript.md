## Gear and timeline
- Started on SP-12, then SP-1200, then MPC60, MPC60 II, then MPC3000 (Dilla himself, Scratch 2006, via Mixdown and Gearspace) [CONFIRMED]. R.J. Rice gave him the SP-1200 and the Akai S950 in 1992 and "that was when he started working a lot with filtering" (Wax Poetics) [CONFIRMED by his own account + Mixdown].
- MPC3000 "on a vast majority of his beats" from 1994 (Mixdown) [CONFIRMED]; he never read the manual, used the time-shift tool to nudge notes (Big Noise) [UNSURE]. Calls the nudges "baby hairs" (4Columns).
- Recorded to three linked ADAT recorders through a Soundtracs Topaz (Gearspace snippet) [UNSURE].
- Minimoog Voyager from Bob Moog, 2002; MicroKorg for pads, leads, bass (Mixdown) [CONFIRMED the Voyager (Wikipedia + Smithsonian)]. Both after 2002, so late for this era.
- Amp Fiddler's basement had "a drum set, the Rhodes, and the MPC" where Slum recorded their first material (DJ Dez, Wax Poetics) [UNSURE one source]. Dilla himself played piano, guitar, drums, cello (Ma Dukes) [CONFIRMED with Wikipedia].

## Drums, swing, grid
- Core idea [CONFIRMED, 6 sources]: kicks, snares and hats each sit off the grid by their own small amount. Snares early, kicks late and "all over the place", hats straight or late on the "ands". Hein measured Get Dis Money: backbeat claps "a tiny bit early", hats on the "and" of beat 1 and 3 late. Soundfly (Say It): on-beat hats straight, upbeat hats nudged back; kicks on the ands delayed with the hats; snares mostly ahead. Soundfly (U-Love): hats and snares all behind.
- Not sloppy: "intended and meticulously executed" (Charnas via Hein) [CONFIRMED with Big Noise "deliberate and organic"].
- Hat straightness: Big Noise says "hi-hats played freehand", Hein/Soundfly say hats straight on the beat and late on ands. Mixed. Owner's rule "hats dead straight" is in this line already and stays.
- Swing %: MPC range 50-75, "66 = triplet"; Dilla's value NOT FOUND. Snare/kick ms: NOT FOUND (the old -18 / +10 are the engine's own numbers; they stay).
- Dirt: Q-Tip: "he would go through phases where he would purposely make it dirty" (Wax Poetics) [UNSURE]; Vox: low end came from cutting highs off the sample (via J Dillo's note) [CONFIRMED with Bilal/Mixdown "gritty low-pass"].
- Drum choices: "nine snares, nine kicks, nine hi-hats" from one break because each has a different dynamic (Tracklib clip, unopened) [UNSURE].

## Samples and instruments, ranked (only what a source names for this era)
1. **Keys / piano / Rhodes** [CONFIRMED]: Rhodes at Amp's (DJ Dez); James Poyser, a piano player, picks harmony as what hit him in Dilla's Brand New Heavies remix (Wax Poetics); Q-Tip: "a real understanding of harmony and melodies"; Questlove's crew learned his chords for Voodoo.
2. **Guitar** [CONFIRMED 2 sources]: samba acoustic guitar on Runnin' (4Columns, Charnas); Dilla also played guitar (Ma Dukes).
3. **Horns** [UNSURE]: Mangione-family horn under Fall in Love (Diamond D, one source).
4. **Vocal / vocoder chops** [CONFIRMED]: Herbie Hancock vocoder on Get Dis Money and another Herbie flip (Hein); loved vocoder vocals. Engine has no vocoder word, so it is left out of the line.
5. **Strings** [NOT FOUND] for this era. Strings appear on Donuts' "Last Donut" (J Dillo's).
6. **Bass**: Q-Tip: "drum-bass thing was dope" (Wax Poetics); Moog bass came after 2002. No kick-locked claim anywhere.
