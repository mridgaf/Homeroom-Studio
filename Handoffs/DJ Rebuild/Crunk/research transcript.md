Read: the hip-hop core runs two ways. Slow grind 73-82 felt (Get Crunk 78, Bia Bia 80, Snap Yo Fingers 82), which is 146-164 on this engine's doubled count. And the radio records sit at 100-105 (Get Low 101, TDFW 100, Yeah! 105). The festival end is 128-140. So "half-time at 140" fits the slow grind and the 140 festival track; it does NOT fit the 100-105 records. Owner pick (see handoff).

## Who made what
- Lil Jon calls TDFW "really a rap song" and describes it as an 808-driven 100 BPM track with synths (107.9 Is Hot interview) [CONFIRMED with Hooktheory/SongBPM 100].
- DJ Snake built the beat around a Redman sample; Lil Jon said the beat was "too crazy for that sample" (Grantland) and re-did it with his voice (Wikipedia, Billboard via search) [CONFIRMED].
- Yeah!: recorded over two days in Miami and one night at Stankonia, Atlanta. Built on a leftover instrumental first given to Petey Pablo (Freek-a-Leek), reworked for Usher (RBMA, Wikipedia) [CONFIRMED].
- Get Low: first chorus was "Let It Go", scrapped; engineer Ray Seay is credited by Lil Jon for the mumbling at the top (TMZ/AOL from the One Song podcast; Mix Online credits Seay as the mixer) [CONFIRMED].
- Some Cut: beat programmed by Lil Jon around a squeaking chair; guitar by Craig Love, bass guitar by LaMarquis Jefferson (Wikipedia) [CONFIRMED one source for the chair, credits from liner data].

## Tempo and feel
- Wikipedia Crunk: Get Crunk is 78 BPM; "spare, low-BPM rhythms" from Miami bass. Recording Arts: "slow grinding tempo". A production guide says 140-160 which is a half-time 70-80 (informal, [UNSURE]).
- Lil Jon: his music is built so a club "goes insane when it goes on" (107.9). He also called the sound "black rock and roll, or black punk-rock" (107.9, Wikipedia infobox influences: Bad Brains, Korn).
- Racine Record on Snap Yo Fingers: "controlled, mid-tempo bounce", "wide-open spaces", minimal synth plucks, snappy handclaps [UNSURE, 2026 retrospective].

## Drums
- Looped, stripped-down drum-machine claps and bass-drum rhythms; sparse, truncated 808 handclaps; TR-808 and 909 the most popular (Wikipedia Crunk) [CONFIRMED with iZotope: Lil Jon "practically made a career" of handclaps; Yeah! named].
- Staccato snare from late-90s Memphis mixtape DJs, Three 6 Mafia (Recording Arts) [UNSURE one source; Wikipedia also credits Memphis].
- Yeah! drum map (Loopcloud, a sample-pack breakdown, not from the producer): kick on 1 and between 1 and 2, clap on 2 and 4, closed hat on eighths in choruses only, open hat on the last eighth, combi cymbal at the start of every two bars. Kick and clap run the whole song [UNSURE, one analysis].
- Lil Jon "reuses the same drums" (forum posts, illmuzik 2005; one post says he called the 808 kit the main Southern ingredient, citing Scratch) [UNSURE forum, Scratch page not found].
- Inside the Credits: "double time hi-hats, tempo maintaining claps" in his programming [UNSURE, one newsletter].
