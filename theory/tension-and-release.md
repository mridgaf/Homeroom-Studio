# Tension and release (the engine of feeling)

Music works like breathing: tighten up, then let go. Almost every trick that
makes a beat feel *good* is really just a controlled way of holding your
breath and then releasing it. Below are the classic moves, plain-English.

## The moves

- **Dominant-to-home pull**: the "V chord" (the chord built on the 5th step,
  E in A minor) is a stretched rubber band; the home chord (i, Am) is letting
  it snap back. The note one key below home (the "leading tone") is the part
  of the stretch your ear feels pulling hardest.
- **Suspension**: hold a note over from the last chord even though the new
  chord has already changed underneath it — it clashes for a beat, then
  drops into place. Feels like a held breath followed by relief.
- **Pedal tone**: keep ONE bass note droning the whole time while the chords
  above it keep changing. Builds pressure like water rising behind a dam;
  finally moving the bass note is opening the floodgates.
- **Lament bass**: the bass walks DOWN one key at a time (A-G-F-E, or every
  single key down A-G#-G-F#-F-E). A centuries-old grief formula — think
  *Hotel California*'s verse, *Hit the Road Jack*.
- **Deceptive cadence (the "rug pull")**: set the listener up expecting to
  land on home (V chord about to resolve to i)... and land somewhere else
  instead (like VI). Stomach-drop feeling — "the story isn't over yet."

## Why it works (the short science version)

Your brain predicts what's coming next in music, the same way it predicts
the next word in a sentence. Emotion shows up in the GAP between what you
expected and what you got — delay the expected chord, deny it, then finally
give it to the listener, and that gap IS the feeling. Sudden loud entrances
after a quiet stripped-down bar, or an unexpected chord you didn't see
coming, are the two most reliable ways to actually give someone chills.

## Use it on the Launchkey

Make bar 4 of your loop the "tension bar": swap in the V chord, hold a
suspended chord (sus4), or just rip the drums out for a beat. When the loop
restarts back on bar 1, it will feel like a release even though nothing
"big" happened — you engineered the breath, not just the notes.
