# Hermes prompt: Dr. Dre 2001-era research (Part 1 of 1)

TASK: Research Dr. Dre's beat-making in his "2001 era" (1999-2003) so a beat machine can copy it. Write a research transcript. Every part must end in numbers or named sounds, not opinions.

WHY: The beat machine's current description of this style is one sentence, and it only covers drums: "surgical West Coast: heavy punchy kick, a crisp hard snare that NEVER leaves 2 and 4, straight-eighth hats, tambourine and cowbell color, sparse disciplined arrangement with an occasional deep sub." It names no era, tempo, swing, instruments, bass or gear. Your job is to fill those gaps.

ERA: Center on the album "2001" (1999), plus his 1999-2003 productions. Tracks to check (verify the credits yourself):
- Still D.R.E., The Next Episode, Forgot About Dre, Xxplosive, What's the Difference, The Watcher
- Eminem - The Real Slim Shady
- Mary J. Blige - Family Affair
- Eve - Let Me Blow Ya Mind
- Busta Rhymes - Break Ya Neck
- 50 Cent - In Da Club
Leave out the G-funk era (1992-1996) unless a source compares the two. If tambourine or cowbell are NOT part of this era, say so.

RULES:
1. Put a source link after every fact.
2. Tag every fact [CONFIRMED] or [UNSURE].
3. Never guess a number. No source = "NOT FOUND".
4. Name the gear when describing a sound ("Minimoog synth bass", "Akai MPC3000 drums"). Only gear a source ties to Dre in this era.
5. If a page won't open, list it under "Blocked links" and move on.
6. Plain words, no filler. Keep quotes under 15 words.

WRITE 9 PARTS, labeled "Part 1 of 9" and so on:

Part 1 - Track table: track | year | artist | credited producers | BPM | key (e.g. "C# minor") | source.

Part 2 - Tempo: slowest, fastest and most common BPM from Part 1.

Part 3 - Swing: swing % per track (MPC-style; 50% = dead straight) where a source gives it. Are the drums locked to the grid or loose?

Part 4 - Drums. One entry each for kick, snare, clap, closed hat, open hat, percussion:
- what it sounds like (short or long, bright or dark, heavy or light)
- where it lands in one 16-step bar, written like "X---X---X---X---" (X = hit), for 2+ tracks, only if a transcription or tutorial shows it
- the gear or source of the sound

Part 5 - Melody and chord instruments (piano, strings, synths, Rhodes, guitar, bells, horns, anything else):
- rank them by how often they show up
- the job each one does (main riff, chord bed, one-note stab, background)
- how it's played (held chords, repeating riff, single-note line)
- played live by a musician, or sampled
- the gear

Part 6 - Key and chords: minor or major, the common keys, how many notes long the riffs are, any chord moves that repeat across tracks.

Part 7 - Bass: what plays it (synth, live bass guitar, 808), long notes or short, does it follow the kick, the gear.

Part 8 - Mix and arrangement:
- how many sounds play at the same time (a count)
- how parts drop in and out across a song
- reverb on the drums: dry or wet?
- saturation or distortion: clean or dirty?
- does the bass duck (dip in volume) when the kick hits?
- how loud and clean the overall mix is

Part 9 - What others say, and who carries it on:
- what his co-producers, engineers and artists said about how he works: Scott Storch, Mike Elizondo, Mel-Man, his engineers on "2001", Eminem, Snoop Dogg, 50 Cent
- producers said to carry on or copy this sound, and what they took from it
