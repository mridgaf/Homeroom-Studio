# Lo-Fi Sample Chop — dusty soul-loop texture

**Accuracy: B** — the filtering/saturation half is fully stock; real vinyl crackle needs a noise sample (easy to add), and tape warble needs a slow pitch LFO trick (step 6).

## The Chain
NN-XT (chops) → MClass EQ (bandwidth cut) → Scream 4 (tape) → CF-101 (subtle warble)

## Steps
1. Chop: load the sample into NN-XT, slice your chops across pads/keys (or use Dr. Octo Rex if it's a REX loop). Root each chop to a comfortable key zone.
2. Play the chops as an instrument — the flip matters more than the effects. Get the pattern right before any processing.
3. MClass EQ: Lo Cut ON, plus high cut — Param 2 at ~9 kHz, -9 dB, wide Q. Old records have no extreme top or bottom; this single move is 50% of "lo-fi."
4. Scream 4: Damage type Tape, Damage 30–45, P2 (speed) low. Compression-like thickening plus soft-clip warmth.
5. Vinyl layer: put an actual vinyl-crackle sample (free online) on its own channel under the loop at -25 dB. Fake crackle from a device fools nobody; a real crackle recording does.
6. Warble: CF-101, rate very slow (~15), delay ~50, feedback 0, sync OFF, dry/wet low. This fakes tape pitch drift. Subtle — if you can point to it, halve it.
7. Bus it with your boom-bap drum bus recipe; shared Scream on the 2-bus marries loop and drums.

## Why this works
Removing highs/lows and adding harmonic clutter mimics generational loss — the ear reads "old media" as "warm and nostalgic."

## Order of operations
EQ before saturation: cutting the top first means Scream's harmonics land in the mids where the warmth lives, not as brittle top.

## Reason-specific trick
NN-XT group feature: set chop groups to mono/legato so overlapping chops cut each other off like an MPC in mono mode.

## RE upgrade path
None required. If chopping is your main workflow, the Beat Map or similar chop-focused RE speeds the workflow but doesn't change the sound.

## Technique you just learned
Bandwidth reduction as a vibe tool — and layering real-world noise instead of synthesizing it.
